Low-rank adaptation~\citep{hu2022lora} has made it cheap to produce many
specialised versions of one base model, and correspondingly attractive to
fold several of them back into a single set of weights. A large family of
merging heuristics now exists for this: task
arithmetic~\citep{ilharco2023task}, sign-election methods such as
TIES~\citep{yadav2023ties}, stochastic sparsification such as
DARE~\citep{yu2024dare}, subspace alignment such as
KnOTS~\citep{stoica2025knots}, and closed-form least-squares approaches in
the RegMean line~\citep{jin2023regmean,lorm2024,regmeanpp2025}.

These methods are evaluated almost entirely by benchmark averages, and the
resulting picture is unstable: the same method can be best on one cohort of
adapters and worst on another, with no way to tell in advance which
situation one is in.

\paragraph{One detail of how the adapters were trained explains a lot of
it.} LoRA factorises each update as $BA$, initialises $A$ at random and $B$
at zero, and trains. Whether the $T$ adapters of a cohort drew \emph{one}
$A$ or $T$ independent ones is a detail almost never reported, and it is
what a single global training seed silently decides. It also determines the
geometry of the merge problem. Across four base models, sharing the draw
moves the median principal cosine between task row spaces from $0.047$ to
$0.995$ (\S\ref{sec:regimes}, Table~\ref{tab:geometry}). The task subspaces
stop being distinguishable directions and become one direction measured
four times.

The mechanism is immediate once stated. $B$ starts at zero, so early
gradients reach $A$ only through a vanishing $B$; $A$ moves little
thereafter, $\|\Delta A\|/\|A\| = 0.16$ to $0.19$ at convergence, and every
task inherits the row space it started with. Nothing about this is a bug in
LoRA, and nothing about it is visible in a training curve or a benchmark
average.

\paragraph{It is invisible to the methods most people use and decisive for
the rest.} We pre-registered the test and report both halves
(\S\ref{sec:methods}). Task arithmetic, TIES, DARE and TVQ show no
systematic effect across twenty base-by-method cells at our $0.01$ nat
resolution, because each returns a bounded-norm near-mean solution that
never enters the ill-conditioned directions: their energy in those
directions is a rounding error. Methods that solve the linear system are a
different story. They reach $35$ to $57$ times the mean task delta with
three quarters of their energy exactly there, and run unregularised they are
$2.1$ to $65$ times worse on a shared-initialisation cohort, $25$ to $117$
times with rank truncation removed. A single Tikhonov term repairs it, on
two solvers of which one is not ours.

\paragraph{Diagnosing it costs a CPU minute.} The quantity above is computed
from the adapter weight files alone: no forward passes, no evaluation data,
no merging. About one minute for a cohort of four rank-16 adapters
(\S\ref{subsec:audit}). A practitioner can run it before choosing a merge
method, which is the only part of this paper we expect anyone to use
directly.

\paragraph{And the regime is common in released adapters.} Every cohort
discussed so far is one we trained ourselves, in both conditions,
deliberately. So we ran the audit over public LoRA cohorts under a sampling
frame registered before any adapter was downloaded: walk the HuggingFace
adapter index by downloads descending, group by publisher and base model,
take the first $50$ cohorts meeting criteria that are all checkable from
metadata, skip none after inspection. \textbf{Seventeen of the $45$ scorable
cohorts, $37.8\%$, are in the collapsed regime}
(\S\ref{subsec:prevalence}). The distribution is bimodal rather than a
continuum: one cohort of $45$ lies between the two populations.

\paragraph{And it reaches accuracy, not just our metric.} A four-order-of
magnitude difference measured in a loss nobody deploys would still be open to
the objection that the loss is the wrong unit. On the same merges, scored on
GSM8K and HumanEval with the repaired scorers, the ridge recovers the shared
arm past the noise gate on four of four bases on both benchmarks. On Mistral
the unregularised shared-cohort merge scores \textbf{zero} on both, with all
$664$ generations non-empty and none discarded, and recovers to $0.46$ and
$0.24$ (\S\ref{subsec:downstream-cond}).

\paragraph{How we found it, and what the theory does and does not do.} The
route here was a rate-distortion analysis, and it is worth being exact about
its role rather than letting a reader infer it. We treat merging as lossy
compression: $T$ task vectors summarised by one weight vector, with the
figure of merit the \emph{worst-task} excess loss, because a merged model
that collapses on a single task is not deployable. \S\ref{sec:lower_bound}
proves a lower bound holding for every merging algorithm, even one given the
curvature data free,
\begin{equation*}
  \underbrace{B^2\Bigl(1 - \frac{\deff}{Tr}\Bigr)}_{\text{irreducible floor}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{c\cdot\frac{B^2\deff}{Tr}\,2^{-2R/\deff}}_{\text{rate term}},
\end{equation*}
with $\deff$ the effective dimension of the union of the task subspaces.
Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

Its operational content in this paper is that it directed attention to
$\bar H$, the operator built from the task subspaces, and to the
conditioning of $\bar H$ in particular. That is the whole of it, and we would
rather write it here than have a reader find it in a limitations list.
\textbf{The bound does not derive any empirical result that follows.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:general} constrains a $w^\star$ carrying no rank
constraint while every merge we run is truncated to rank $r$, so at that
step it bears on our measurements by analogy and not by derivation
(\S\ref{subsec:conditioning}). Its floor term is vacuous on real data. Its
rate term rests on an assumption we state and do not prove
(Assumption~\ref{asm:epr}), and our attempt to measure the predicted
exponent has too little dynamic range to do so (\S\ref{subsec:w3}). We
think the lens earned its keep, because nothing else we tried pointed at
$\bar H$, but it is a lens: everything in \S\ref{sec:regimes} and
\S\ref{sec:methods} stands or falls on its own measurements.

\paragraph{The floor is vacuous, and we say so.} The natural next step is to
measure the floor on real cohorts and report how much of the observed merging
loss is irreducible. We did, and the answer is that \textbf{the floor is
exactly zero} on all four base models under every initialisation scheme we
tried. That is a statement about a rank: the floor vanishes precisely when the
task row spaces are in direct sum, and $T$ rank-$r$ adapters with $Tr \leq d$
are linearly independent even when their subspaces are nearly collinear.
Near-collinear is not collinear.

It is also, deliberately, a statement no finite-precision merge can act on.
Evaluating the floor needs a pseudoinverse of a matrix whose condition number
reaches $8.3\times10^{4}$, so at a loose tolerance the shared-initialisation
cohort returns $0.16$ to $0.37\,B^2$ rather than zero
(\S\ref{subsec:floor-zero}). A cohort with a genuinely positive floor and one
with a zero floor are numerically indistinguishable, which is the first sign
that the floor is not the quantity to be looking at.

We report this prominently because an earlier version of this work claimed
otherwise, deriving a floor of $0.745B^2$ for one cohort against $0.012B^2$
for another. That difference was an artifact of substituting a soft
participation-ratio surrogate into a formula derived for a rank
(\S\ref{subsec:floor-zero}). For LoRA merging as practised, the floor term
carries no operational content.

\paragraph{What the collapsed geometry does control.} A zero floor says an
exact interpolator exists; it says nothing about its norm, and that is where
the two regimes part. Under a shared draw the exact interpolator is $43$ to
$64$ times larger than the mean task delta; under independent draws it is
$T$. That $T$ is not fitted, it is the orthogonal-subspace value, and the
measurement returns it to within $1.4\%$ at both $T = 3$ and $T = 4$. The
condition number of $\bar H$ moves correspondingly, from about $1.5$ to
between $1.8\times10^{4}$ and $8.3\times10^{4}$
(\S\ref{sec:regimes}, Figure~\ref{fig:two-regimes}).

We lead on the principal cosines rather than on $\kappa(\bar H)$ throughout,
and the reason is a limitation of our own construction: the cosines are
properties of the row spaces alone and hold under any curvature model,
whereas $\kappa(\bar H)$ depends on the specialisation $H_t = P_{V_t}$ we
adopt and would take a different value under a Fisher-weighted $H_t$
(\S\ref{subsec:conditioning}). The four-orders-of-magnitude figure is real
and is a calibrated index of subspace overlap; it is not the condition
number any particular solver encounters, and we do not report it as one.

So the claim is narrow and, we think, useful: cohort conditioning is
invisible to the merging methods most people use and decisive for the ones
that solve a system, and one ridge term is the difference.

\paragraph{What did not survive.} We pre-registered several tests and lost
several of them. Our own rate-distortion encoder's advantage does not survive
the move from shared-initialisation cohorts to properly initialised ones. A
pre-registered control we designed ourselves withdrew our headline claim that
published merging benchmarks are confounded by initialisation geometry; a
second control, registered afterwards and identified as secondary, reinstates
it only as provisional (\S\ref{subsec:q1}). A rate-exponent falsification we
previously reported turned out to rest on an asymptote computed at a different
ridge value, and is withdrawn; the refitted sweep turns out not to have the
dynamic range to measure the exponent at all, so we withdraw the one in-band
fit as well (\S\ref{subsec:w3}). One of our four adapters never learned its
task, so our cohorts are $T=4$ nominal and $3$ effective, and we report every
geometric quantity at both. All eight pre-registrations are reproduced verbatim
in Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}, included from the source files rather than
summarised, with the commit ordering that makes them checkable.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A closed-form lower bound on worst-task NLL excess for rank-$r$
  merging (Theorem~\ref{thm:general}) with arbitrary task-dependent
  curvature, and a matching construction (Theorem~\ref{thm:achv}). The
  floor term is unconditional; the rate term holds under an explicitly
  stated assumption on the entropy power (Assumption~\ref{asm:epr}), which
  we do not prove and do not absorb into a constant. Its role in what
  follows is the one stated above: it identifies $\bar H$ as the object to
  measure, and does not derive the measurements.
\item The finding that the bound's floor term is \emph{vacuous} for LoRA
  cohorts in general position, together with the exact instance form
  $B^2 - \tauH^\top\bar H\tauH$ that a practitioner should evaluate instead
  of the prior-averaged expression.
\item The identification of the conditioning of $\bar H$, rather than the
  floor, as what cohort initialisation controls, measured across four base
  models and four cohorts, with an audit that reports it in one CPU minute.
\item A pre-registered prevalence estimate for that regime in adapters we
  did not train: under a sampling frame fixed before any download, $37.8\%$
  of $45$ public LoRA cohorts are collapsed, and the distribution is bimodal
  rather than continuous (\S\ref{subsec:prevalence}).
\item A pre-registered demonstration that this conditioning is invisible to
  standard merging heuristics and decisive for solve-based ones, on two
  solvers, at $n = 3$ cohorts in \emph{both} regimes, and with the mechanism
  measured: the heuristics never place more than a rounding error of their
  norm in the ill-conditioned directions and the unregularised solvers place
  three quarters of it there.
\item A pre-registered demonstration that the effect reaches downstream
  accuracy and is not an artifact of the loss we measure it in: on Mistral
  the unregularised shared-cohort merge scores zero on GSM8K and on
  HumanEval, with no scorer discards, and one ridge term recovers it
  (\S\ref{subsec:downstream-cond}).
\item A negative result for DARE composed with task arithmetic, with a
  mechanism that predicts its own functional form, together with a
  pre-registered demonstration that it does \emph{not} extend to DARE
  composed with TIES, which bounds where the negative result applies.
\end{enumerate}

We also correct several claims from earlier drafts of this work. Those
corrections are not contributions, and we do not present them as such; they
are reported because the results they replace have circulated, and because
the pre-registrations that forced them are the reason we trust what is left.
