# Rotation-based LoRA merging is already crowded; rate-distortion framing is the real opening

**The two proposals face very different competitive landscapes.** A "Hadamard-rotation / incoherence-processing" approach to LoRA adapter merging is largely pre-empted: at least four papers between October 2024 and February 2026 — KnOTS (ICLR 2025), TSPA (OpenReview 2025), DO-Merging (NeurIPS 2025), and ARM/Streaming Merging (arXiv 2602.03237) — already merge LoRA adapters by rotating, orthogonalizing, or SVD-aligning task updates before averaging. Any new rotation-based merging paper would need a very sharp differentiator to survive review. In contrast, an **information-theoretic / rate-distortion framing of model merging is genuinely open**: despite dozens of empirical merging papers, no published work poses merging as a distortion-rate problem with matching lower bounds, even though the toolkit (TurboQuant, QJL, Shannon-style bounds via Yao's minimax) has been successfully transplanted to adjacent problems like KV-cache compression. This asymmetry should drive the research agenda — reframe the "rotation for LoRA merging" work around distortion-rate optimality rather than as a standalone method, or drop it in favor of the information-theoretic angle.

The sections below map the competitive landscape, identify where each proposed contribution sits relative to prior art, and recommend the strongest defensible positioning.

## The rotation-for-LoRA-merging space is already four papers deep

The core intuition behind Proposal 2 — "LoRA adapters interfere because their update subspaces are misaligned; rotate them into a shared basis before merging" — was articulated by **Stoica et al.'s KnOTS (ICLR 2025)** over a year ago. KnOTS concatenates the LoRA task-updates column-wise, takes a joint SVD, and merges in the aligned `V^T` space with any standard method (TIES, DARE, Task Arithmetic) on top. It improves LoRA merging by up to **4.3% across eight vision benchmarks** and has become the reference baseline. Three subsequent papers tighten the idea in different directions:

- **TSPA (Leveraging Rotation Symmetry for Efficient LoRA Merging, 2025)** frames merging as Stiefel-manifold optimization of per-adapter rotation matrices, using a "comparison with an average model" trick to cut complexity from quadratic to linear in the number of adapters, with dual macro-functional and micro-parameter alignment objectives.
- **DO-Merging (NeurIPS 2025)** decouples magnitude and direction, then applies data-free layer-wise orthogonal perturbations to minimize cross-task interference, reporting >3% gains on vision LoRA merges and consistent improvements on Llama-3-8B and Qwen-14B.
- **ARM / Streaming Merging (Feb 2026, arXiv 2602.03237)** derives activation-guided rotation matrices that "approximate gradient-induced directional shifts," treating merging coefficients as learning rates on the Stiefel manifold — this is essentially your rotation-based merging story, already published two months ago.
- **Core Space (Panariello et al., 2025)** proves that merging rank-r LoRAs in a projected "core space" achieves zero alignment error via Lagrange multipliers on the Stiefel constraint, and is provably more efficient than KnOTS.

A fifth directly adjacent paper, **TARA-Merging**, explicitly raises the "directional anisotropy" problem as the central open question in LoRA merging — i.e., even after subspace alignment, task losses are unequally sensitive to different LoRA directions. Any rotation-based proposal now needs to either (a) solve the anisotropy problem TARA raises, (b) offer a provable optimality guarantee the others lack, or (c) operate in a regime none of them cover. **"We rotate LoRA adapters before merging and it helps" is no longer a contribution.**

A further complication is the **November 2025 large-scale empirical study** (arXiv 2511.21437) which found that across four LLM families, twelve checkpoints per base, and sixteen benchmarks, **"only Task Arithmetic reliably produces constructive interference"** when merging randomly-sampled checkpoints — subspace-based methods including KnOTS-style alignment "often underperform or fail" because update directions in real LLM fine-tunes do not form the stable subspaces those methods assume. This means the rotation-merging literature has a reproducibility / scope problem you would have to confront directly.

## Hadamard-specific twists are mostly borrowed from quantization literature

The specific move of using **Hadamard rotations to induce incoherence before merging** has obvious provenance: QuIP/QuIP# and QTIP use Hadamard incoherence processing to flatten weight distributions before scalar quantization, and **TurboQuant (Zandieh et al., April 2025)** proves that random rotation + optimal per-coordinate scalar quantization achieves distortion within a factor of ≈2.7 of the Shannon lower bound, with a formal proof using Yao's minimax principle. **RoLoRA (2024)** already combines Hadamard rotation with LoRA fine-tuning specifically to eliminate activation outliers for W4A4 quantization, and demonstrates rotation-aware two-stage merging of rotation matrices with LoRA weights. **Walsh-Hadamard grouped sequency rotation** (Choi et al., May 2025) further refines block-diagonal Hadamard structure for quantization error control.

The natural question a reviewer will ask is: **if Hadamard incoherence is so useful for LoRA+quantization (RoLoRA) and for vector-quantization under rate-distortion bounds (TurboQuant), why hasn't anyone applied it to LoRA merging?** The honest answer is that two recent papers nearly cross that bridge: **Task Vector Quantization (Kim et al., ICCV 2025)** quantizes task vectors for memory-efficient merging and observes their narrow weight range makes low-bit quantization especially clean — but it uses neither rotation nor distortion-rate analysis. **1bit-Merging (2025)** does module-wise 1-bit quantization of task vectors with routing, again without rotation or rate-distortion framing. This is exactly the gap.

## Information-theoretic bounds for merging are genuinely unclaimed

My searches for "rate-distortion" crossed with "model merging," "task vector," or "task arithmetic" returned **zero matches** in the merging literature itself. The closest hits are:

1. **Task Vector Bases (Jang et al., 2025, arXiv 2502.01015)**, which "assumes fine-tuned weights lie in a thin Gaussian shell" — a geometric but not information-theoretic assumption.
2. **On Task Vectors and Gradients (Aug 2025, arXiv 2508.16082)**, which connects task vectors to negative gradients at one epoch of full-batch GD, bounding the second-order error for feed-forward networks. This is the current state-of-the-art *theoretical* analysis of task arithmetic, and it is **not information-theoretic** — it is a Taylor-expansion argument.
3. **ATM (Alternating Tuning and Merging, 2024)**, which reinterprets task arithmetic as one noisy GD step toward a joint-task optimum, again without rate-distortion machinery.

Meanwhile, the **tools to do rate-distortion analysis of merging exist and are sharp**: TurboQuant's Theorem 3 gives a Shannon-style lower bound on any randomized vector quantizer via Yao's minimax, matched up to a constant ≈2.7 by the Haar-rotation + Lloyd-Max construction. Ortiz-Jimenez et al.'s **weight disentanglement** framework (NeurIPS 2023 oral) provides a rigorous function-space notion of when distinct weight directions govern disjoint input regions — this is essentially a *distortion* condition waiting for a rate counterpart. And there is a **classical 1980s result** by Linial ("The information theoretic bound is good for merging," SIAM J. Computing) that — though it concerns list-merging for ordered sets, not neural networks — could provide rhetorical framing for the title and intro.

The natural formulation that no one has written: given T task vectors {τ₁,…,τ_T} with bounded norms and per-task performance functions L_t, **what is the minimum number of bits required to store a merged model that achieves distortion ≤ ε on every L_t?** A lower bound via a packing/Fano argument, combined with a Hadamard-rotation + scalar-quantization achievability scheme (directly adapted from TurboQuant), would yield the first rate-distortion theorem for model merging. This is the strongest defensible contribution in the proposal space.

## Recommended repositioning of the two proposals

The evidence supports collapsing the two proposals into **one information-theoretic paper**, with rotation as a technique in service of matching the bound rather than the headline contribution. Concretely:

1. **Lead with a lower bound.** Prove a Shannon-style lower bound on the bits-per-parameter required to merge T LoRA adapters while preserving ε-distortion on each task, using a Fano / minimax argument analogous to TurboQuant's Theorem 3. Frame merging as lossy source coding of the set of task vectors under a worst-case task-loss distortion measure. This is **wide-open territory** in the merging literature.
2. **Show that Hadamard rotation + quantized merging matches the bound up to a constant.** Borrow TurboQuant's construction directly: rotate each task vector by a Haar (or structured-Hadamard) orthogonal matrix, apply per-coordinate Lloyd-Max quantization, average in rotated space, rotate back. The key claim — that this is order-optimal — is defensible because the same construction is already proven near-optimal for vector quantization.
3. **Position against KnOTS/TSPA/DO-Merging/ARM/Core Space as baselines, not competitors.** Your contribution is the *rate* side (how few bits are necessary and sufficient); their contributions are on the *distortion* side (given full precision, how to align). Experimentally, show you match their accuracy at 3-4 bits per task-vector coordinate, reproducing the TVQ/1bit-Merging empirical regime but with provable guarantees those papers lack.
4. **Confront the November 2025 negative result directly.** The Systematic Study paper (arXiv 2511.21437) claims subspace methods fail at LLM scale under random checkpoint sampling; your rate-distortion framing explains *why* — random task vectors have near-maximal rate under any distortion measure, which is exactly when subspace alignment cannot help but rotation-based quantization still can.

## What to drop and what to keep

**Drop**: Any framing of "rotation-based LoRA merging" as a novel method. Drop the "incoherence processing improves merging" framing if presented as empirical contribution only — RoLoRA and the four rotation-merging papers collectively own this claim.

**Keep and sharpen**: The rate-distortion lower bound (genuinely new), the information-theoretic achievability via Hadamard + scalar quantization (new in the merging setting), and connections to the weight-disentanglement literature for the distortion measure. The classical Linial result provides a nice rhetorical anchor for the title.

**Add as defensive moves**: A clear experimental comparison against TVQ (ICCV 2025), 1bit-Merging, KnOTS, TSPA, Core Space, and Task Arithmetic at matched bit budgets; an ablation against plain random Gaussian rotation (TurboQuant uses Haar, not Hadamard, so showing Hadamard-specific benefits — e.g., FFT-speed — is important); and an honest discussion of when the lower bound is loose (likely when task vectors have structure, which the bound does not exploit).

## Conclusion: the opening is theoretical, not methodological

The research community has spent 18 months converging on rotation- and subspace-alignment techniques for LoRA merging from multiple angles — SVD-based (KnOTS, Core Space), Stiefel-optimization (TSPA, ARM), and orthogonalization-based (DO-Merging) — while leaving the information-theoretic foundations of merging almost entirely unexplored. **The right bet is to treat merging as lossy source coding**, prove a matching lower bound, and use Hadamard-rotation-based quantized merging as the achievability construction. This flips the proposal's risk profile: instead of competing with five rotation-merging papers on empirical accuracy, you compete with zero on theoretical optimality, while still getting to publish the Hadamard construction — just as a corollary rather than as the main event.

The most aggressive timeline risk is that someone at Google Research (Zandieh, Mirrokni et al., the TurboQuant team) notices the same gap — their VQ machinery transplants so cleanly to task-vector merging that this paper essentially writes itself from their Theorems 1-3. Moving quickly matters.
