Low-rank adapters (LoRA) fine-tuned from one base model are routinely merged
into a single model. We show that a detail almost never reported, whether the
adapters of a cohort started from one random draw of the LoRA $A$ factor or
from independent draws, decides whether a whole class of merges works. With a
shared draw, which one training seed or the defaults of Unsloth and
LLaMA-Factory arrange, the task subspaces collapse: their median principal
cosine exceeds 0.99, against 0.05 for independent draws, on four base models.
The initialisation seed alone decides this, and training every task on from
one adapter or initialising with PiSSA collapses a cohort without a shared
seed. On collapsed cohorts, WUDI-Merging, a published data-free method run as
its authors run it, leaves a worst-task loss excess 4.7 to 47.6 times larger
than on independent cohorts, and on GSM8K it scores 0.03 against 0.70 on
Llama-3.1-8B. A data-free form of RegMean and our closed-form encoder fail as
well. All three solve a least-squares problem built from the adapters alone.
Averaging and sparsifying merges, and solvers built from data, are unaffected.
A ridge recovers most of the loss on three of four bases, and RegMean's shipped
regulariser is too weak to. A first-order theory accounts for the conditioning
within 0.10\% and predicts it on eight cohorts trained after it was fixed. An
audit that reads the adapter files in a CPU minute finds the collapse in 18 of
45 public LoRA cohorts. Every confirmatory test was pre-registered, and the
predictions that failed are reported.
