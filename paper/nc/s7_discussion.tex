\paragraph{What to do in practice.} The results reduce to three cases.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item If the merge averages or sparsifies the updates, or is a solver whose
  matrix is built from data, the initialisation does not matter and no action
  is needed.
\item If the merge solves against a matrix built from the adapters alone, run
  Algorithm~\ref{alg:audit} first. On a collapsed cohort, add an isotropic
  ridge, start near $\lambda = 0.01$ and sweep it; do not rely on RegMean's
  shipped $\alpha$.
\item If several tasks are fine-tuned with Unsloth or LLaMA-Factory at default
  settings, or with PiSSA, OLoRA or MiLoRA initialisation, the adapters are
  collapsed. Setting a different seed per task avoids it for the first two and
  not for the last three. Designs that share $A$ deliberately, such as
  HydraLoRA or federated LoRA with a frozen or aggregated $A$, are collapsed by
  construction.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Predictions that failed.} Six registered predictions did not hold.
TSV-M was not exposed (\S\ref{subsec:results}). Solver failures on public
cohorts were not specific to the collapse, and a data-free ridge rule did worse
than a fixed start (\S\ref{subsec:against}). The heuristics' slight lean
towards the shared arm persisted at three cohorts per arm
(\S\ref{subsec:results}). RegMean with activation Grams produced solutions
$2.2$ to $6.5$ times the mean task update rather than at most three, equally on
both arms, because the activation Grams are themselves badly conditioned
($10^{4}$ to $10^{7}$) whatever the initialisation. The retrained translation
adapter improved but did not become the best adapter at translation.

\paragraph{Relation to the earlier version.} The preprint this paper
supersedes~\citep{pathak2026rd} reported that our closed-form encoder had the
lowest worst-task loss of ten methods on all four bases. That advantage came
from the shared initialisation of its cohorts: on independent cohorts the
encoder beats the best baseline on no base. It also reported that worst-task
NLL tracks the downstream ranking of methods. With five or six methods that
correlation cannot be measured reliably, and on HumanEval its sign is wrong,
which is why every accuracy claim here is measured in accuracy.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The controlled experiments use four base models of 7
to 9 billion parameters and four tasks, one of which did not train properly,
so the merged models contain one weak member; the task-count and rank sweep is
on one base. Larger models, other architectures and other adapter types are
untested. The value of $\kappa(\bar H)$ depends on the projector curvature
model, while the principal cosines, which carry the audit, are independent of
it. The theory is a
leading-order expansion verified numerically, not a bound. Training and
evaluation data overlap on two tasks ($13$ to $16\%$ for instruction following
and $9$ to $12\%$ for code), so absolute scores are optimistic; every
comparison here is between the two initialisation arms on the same data, and
on the disjoint subset the ordering of methods is unchanged in $19$ of $20$
cases. The library reading is of pinned versions, and defaults can change.
Finally, the public-cohort merges show that adapter-built solvers fail often on
released adapters for reasons we have not identified; those failures should
not be attributed to the collapse, and such solvers should not be used on
public adapters without checking the result.
