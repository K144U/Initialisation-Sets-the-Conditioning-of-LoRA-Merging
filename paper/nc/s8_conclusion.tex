Whether the adapters of a LoRA cohort share their initial $A$ is fixed before
training starts, is decided by a seed or a library default, and is almost never
reported. It changes the cohort's geometry by orders of magnitude: shared
starts leave the task subspaces nearly collinear, and a first-order theory in
the adapters' displacement accounts for the resulting conditioning of the
merge operator to within $0.10\%$ and predicts it across training regimes. The
collapsed regime is common: in our own pipelines, in two popular fine-tuning
stacks at their defaults, in PiSSA initialisation whatever the seed, in
adapters trained on from one parent, and in $18$ of $45$ public cohorts. Its
consequences for merging are specific. Averaging, sparsifying and data-built
merges are unaffected. Merges that solve a least-squares problem built from
the adapters alone fail, and that class includes WUDI-Merging run as its
authors run it. A ridge repairs the closed-form solvers on most bases but
cannot be set without data, so before such a merge the first step is a
one-minute audit of the adapter files, which tells which case a cohort is in.

\paragraph{Code and data availability.} The code, configurations, per-cell
results, pre-registration documents and the commit history that orders them
are public at \url{\repourl}, including the audit script
(\texttt{measure\_\allowbreak subspace\_\allowbreak geometry.py}).
