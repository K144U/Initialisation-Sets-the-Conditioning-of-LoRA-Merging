This appendix collects the $T$-scaling details behind
\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}: the sweep setup, the log-$T$ slope fits,
the TIES sign-election probe, and the three Yi-specific artifacts the
cross-model sweep resolves.

\paragraph{Setup.} For each base the authors trained three additional rank-$16$
adapters (CodeAlpaca, Dolly, XSum) and merged them with the four
$T = 4$-matrix adapters. Six methods span the matrix: TA, TIES, DARE,
KnOTS, TVQ$_2$, and rd-encoder ridge at the base-optimal $\lambda$. For
each $T$ the authors draw one fixed nested chain plus three random subsets at
$T \in \{2, 4\}$ and the all-$7$-tasks subset at $T = 7$; each cell
evaluates worst-task NLL excess at $n_{\text{eval}} = 1000$. Total:
$162$ merge cells ($54$ per base, identical subset composition across
bases).

\paragraph{Log-$T$ slopes.} Table~\ref{tab:e6-slopes} fits
$\text{worst-excess}(T) = a + b\log T$ per (base, method). The
synthetic estimate (\S\ref{subsec:exp-setup}) gives
$b \approx 1/\sqrt{r} = 0.25$ at $r = 16$; measured slopes are
positive with the functional form holding at $R^2 \geq 0.93$ on $16$
of $18$ all-cells fits (the exceptions are the Yi and Mistral TVQ$_2$
fits), with the empirical $\rho$ between half and two-thirds of the
synthetic estimate on Llama/Mistral. Rd-ridge has the lowest and most
base-invariant slope.

\paragraph{The TIES sign-election probe.} To test the conjecture that
sign-election consensus degrades faster on Yi than Llama, the authors ran a
per-coordinate sign-vote audit on both $T = 7$ cohorts: trim each
adapter's $\Delta$ at density $0.2$ (the TIES default), stack the
trimmed deltas, and tally per-coordinate votes plus per-task win-share
(fraction of coordinates where that task's sign matches the elected
sign). Table~\ref{tab:ties-probe} reports the result: vote-split
structure is nearly identical between the bases (rejecting the
literal ``consensus degrades'' conjecture) but the \emph{per-task
win-share range} is $2.8\times$ wider on Yi. The refined mechanism is
a \emph{directionally biased} consensus, not a degraded one: on
Yi-Chat, six adapters sharing instruction-following structure vote in
a common subspace, and the code adapter (Magicoder, win share $0.776$)
and the near-saturated Alpaca adapter ($0.785$) are systematically
outvoted; on Llama the per-task votes are nearly uniform
($0.805$--$0.832$). The probe is CPU-only and takes a few minutes per
cohort.

\paragraph{Three Yi-specific artifacts the cross-model sweep rules
out.} \textbf{(A) The TIES inversion at $T = 7$ is Yi-specific,}
driven by the win-share concentration above; on Llama TIES at $T = 7$
is third-best of six and on Mistral second-best, as the pre-registered
threshold test of \S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling} predicted.
\textbf{(B) TVQ-$b{=}2$'s flat-in-$T$ behavior is Yi-specific:} slope
$-0.001$ on Yi all-cells vs.\ $+0.119$ on Llama; the
Appendix~\ref{app:b2} dip is not a $T$-robust mechanism in general.
\textbf{(C) The Alpaca shallow-adapter artifact is Yi-specific:} Yi's
$T = 4$ all-cells rd-ridge regression ($0.084$ vs.\ TA $0.067$) is
driven entirely by Alpaca, whose base-to-$\tau$ headroom on Yi-Chat is
just $0.114$ (vs.\ $0.236$--$0.554$ for the other adapters), so any
merge perturbation is large \emph{relative} to that headroom; per-layer
$\Delta$-cosine analysis confirms Alpaca is the most isolated adapter
of the cohort (mean cosine $0.028$). On Llama the same adapter has
headroom $0.246$ and no artifact appears. All three are symptoms of
one condition, \emph{Yi-Chat is near-saturated on
instruction-following tasks}, which is the basis of recommendation
R2: renormalize excess by per-task headroom, or exclude near-saturated
adapters, before comparing methods on a heavily chat-tuned base.
