\paragraph{What we think survives.} The bound and its matching construction,
which are mathematics and do not depend on any empirical outcome here. The
observation that the bound's floor term is \emph{vacuous} for LoRA cohorts in
general position, together with the exact instance form
$B^2 - \tauH^\top\bar H\tauH$ that should be evaluated in its place. The
identification of $\kappa(\bar H)$ as the quantity cohort initialisation
actually controls, measured across four base models and four cohorts, with an
amplification of exactly $T$ under independent initialisation reproduced at
two values of $T$. And the pre-registered finding that this conditioning is
invisible to standard merging heuristics and decisive for solve-based ones.

\paragraph{What does not.} Our own encoder is not distinguishable from TIES on
half the bases once cohorts are properly initialised, and worse on one. The
claim that published merging benchmarks are confounded by initialisation
geometry was withdrawn by a control we wrote ourselves. Our earlier report
that the finite-rate exponent is falsified is withdrawn: its two supporting
slopes were fitted against an asymptote computed at a different ridge value,
and against matched asymptotes the question is unresolved rather than settled
either way. We have left all of these at full strength rather than retreating
to the subset that flatters the method, because the conditioning result is
only worth anything if the reader can believe we would have reported it the
other way.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The metric's resolution is about $0.001$ nats, and several of
  our differences are near it.} A zero-mean perturbation changing the merged
  delta by $37$ to $57\%$ in Frobenius norm costs roughly that much. Any
  comparison in this paper at the $0.005$ threshold should be read with that
  in mind; it is why we decline to call two of the four bases in
  Table~\ref{tab:q1} losses. The conditioning results are not affected, since
  their margins run from $0.09$ to $9.6$ nats.
\item \textbf{We cannot measure whether NLL excess predicts downstream
  accuracy.} The Spearman correlations available to us are computed over the
  five or six methods in a row, and under the null
  $\mathrm{SD}(\rho) = 1/\sqrt{n-1} = 0.45$ at $n=6$. Every value we can
  compute, in either direction, is within about $1.5$ standard deviations of
  zero. Values we previously reported as evidence were not evidence.
  Settling it needs roughly $25$ points per base.
\item \textbf{The finite-rate exponent is unresolved, not confirmed.} Against
  matched asymptotes one base fits at $-2.10$ ($R^2 = 0.98$) against a
  predicted $-2$, one at $-3.50$, and two converge to their asymptote by
  $b = 3$ to $4$, leaving too little dynamic range to fit over our grid. A
  finer low-rate grid would settle it; we deliberately have not chosen one
  after seeing which would produce a fit.
\item \textbf{Our KnOTS baseline was a no-op.} With the default inner
  combination it reduces algebraically to task arithmetic and matched it to
  four decimals. We report this as an erratum about our own baseline; we have
  not checked the reference implementation and claim nothing about published
  KnOTS. The consequence is that no subspace-alignment method is represented
  in our comparison.
\item \textbf{One adapter in four never learned its task}, on every base and
  in both arms, so our cohorts are $T=4$ nominal and $3$ effective
  (\S\ref{subsec:scope}). Every geometric quantity is reported at both $T$
  and the contrast is unchanged, but the merged models do contain an
  untrained member, and no claim about merging $T$ well-trained tasks is
  supported by this data.
\item \textbf{The ridge sweep has one cohort per arm.} The pre-registration
  specified a $2\times\mathrm{SE}$ noise gate that we therefore could not
  compute, and only the tie threshold was applied. The margins are far above
  any plausible noise, but the gate is missing and we say so.
\item \textbf{Train and evaluation splits overlap} by about $14.5\%$ on the
  instruction task and $10\%$ on the code task, because the two draws use
  different seeds over one pool. Absolute numbers are optimistic. Every
  method sees identical data, so we do not believe comparisons are affected,
  but no absolute number here is a held-out number.
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:general}'s constant is imported, not derived.}
  Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} isolates the step: we do not prove that the
  entropy-power-to-variance ratio is bounded below uniformly, and transfer
  TurboQuant's constant to our conditional setting as an assumption.
\item \textbf{Scope.} $T=4$, $r=16$, four base models in the 7B to 9B range,
  language only, worst-task NLL excess. $Tr = 64$ is far below $d$, so the
  regime where $Tr$ approaches $d$, and where $\deff = Tr$ must fail, is
  untouched. No rank sweep and no $T$ sweep on real adapters.
\item \textbf{One pre-registered control has a known specification defect}
  (\S\ref{subsec:withdrawn}), which we did not act on, because acting on it
  after seeing which way it fell is what pre-registration exists to prevent.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implications for practice.} The recommendation is conditional and
we state it that way. If you merge with a method that solves a linear system,
audit your cohort's conditioning and regularise: unregularised, the same merge
can be $65$ times worse on an ill-conditioned cohort, and one ridge term
removes the difference. If you merge with task arithmetic, TIES, DARE or a
similar heuristic, our data says the cohort's conditioning will not affect
you, and we found no measurable benefit to changing how the adapters were
initialised. For anyone benchmarking merging methods, report $\kappa(\bar H)$
and the principal cosines alongside the results, and report more than one
initialisation draw, because we find single-draw method rankings are not
reproducible.

\paragraph{Future work.} A margin-aware version of the stability control is
the obvious next pre-registration and may reinstate the claim we withdrew. A
finer low-rate grid would settle the exponent. Checking whether the reference
KnOTS implementation shares the degeneracy ours did would establish whether
that finding is about the field or only about our code. And the regime where
$Tr$ approaches $d$, where the floor must stop being vacuous, is where this
bound would start to have operational content; reaching it needs either many
more tasks or much higher rank.
