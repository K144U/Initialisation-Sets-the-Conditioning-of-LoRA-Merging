\subsection{Practical recommendations}
\label{sec:practical-recommendations}

The lower bound of \S\ref{sec:lower_bound} and the encoder of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder} are theoretical contributions; we close
\S\ref{sec:experiments} with a five-rule decision tree distilled from
the empirical results of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}--\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}.

\paragraph{R1 --- Default to rd-encoder ridge at the base-optimal
$\lambda$.}
On every base we tested (\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}, \S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}),
rd-encoder ridge ties or beats Task Arithmetic and the TIES family at
$T = 4$ on NLL excess, and \emph{strictly} beats them at $T = 7$ on
Llama-3.1-8B with a widening margin (ratio rd-ridge/TA $= 0.84 \to
0.68 \to 0.52$ at $T = 2, 4, 7$). The base-optimal $\lambda$ shifts
mildly with architecture: $\lambda = 0.05$ for Llama-3.1, $\lambda =
0.13$ for Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and Yi-1.5-9B. Re-tuning is one
inexpensive log-scale sweep ($\sim 30$ minutes for $5$ values per
base). The rd-encoder log-$T$ slope is nearly base-invariant
($0.070$ on Yi, $0.065$ on Llama; Table~\ref{tab:e6-slopes}), making
it the most $T$-stable method we observed.

\paragraph{R2 --- Audit the base-saturation regime before trusting
\textsc{nll}-based method comparisons.}
On heavily instruction-tuned chat-style bases, the per-task NLL
headroom from base to $\tau_t$ is compressed, and method comparisons
on \textsc{nll} excess can amplify per-task asymmetries that do not
correspond to deployable quality differences. On Yi-1.5-9B-Chat we
measured Alpaca headroom $= 0.114$ versus $0.236$--$0.554$ for other
E6 adapters; the resulting all-cells $T = 4$ rd-encoder result
\emph{regressed} relative to TA ($0.084$ vs $0.067$) entirely because
of the Alpaca-specific excess inflation
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}). On Llama-3.1 the same Alpaca adapter
has headroom $0.246$ and the corresponding T = 4 cell shows rd-encoder
ridge \emph{wins} ($0.104$ vs TA's $0.153$). The practical guidance:
on chat-tuned bases, either (a) renormalize per-task NLL excess by
per-task headroom ($\Phi_t / (\mathrm{base}_t - \tau_t)$), or (b)
exclude near-saturated adapters from the cohort, before declaring a
method winner. A signal that you are in the saturated regime: the
\textsc{ties} per-task sign-election win-share range exceeds $\sim 0.05$
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}, Table~\ref{tab:ties-probe}); we observed
$0.077$ on Yi-Chat, $0.028$ on Llama-Instruct, and $0.0326$ on
Mistral (below $0.05$, but inside the $[0.025, 0.075]$ Td2 ambiguity
window --- the pre-registered third base, R3).

\paragraph{R3 --- If you cannot use rd-encoder ridge, use TIES or
TVQ-$b{=}2$, not TA, and avoid TIES on saturated bases.}
TIES and TVQ-$b{=}2$ both implement implicit sign-election and produce
sparser, lower-variance merges than TA at $T = 4$
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}). On NLL excess, TIES wins on $3$ of $4$
bases at $T = 4$ but inverts on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat at $T = 7$ (becomes the
\emph{worst} method, \S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}); the inversion is
driven by Yi's per-task sign-election win-share concentration
(directionally biased consensus, not degraded consensus). We
pre-registered this win-share threshold as a falsifiable predictor
(commit \texttt{3582799}, before the Mistral sweep ran):
Mistral-7B, whose win-share range $0.0326$ sits inside the ambiguity
window, was predicted \emph{ambiguous} at $T = 7$. The sweep confirms
it --- TIES second-best of six ($0.152$, $0.092$ nats below TA),
neither inverting to worst nor winning --- so the rule earns
predictive, not merely descriptive, status
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}, Appendix~\ref{app:sign-election-threshold}).
On Llama and
when the saturation signal of R2 is absent, TIES at density $= 0.2$ is
a solid default. On HumanEval pass@1
(Table~\ref{tab:b4-humaneval}, \S\ref{subsec:exp-downstream-gsm8k}),
TVQ-$b{=}2$ is the \emph{best} method on three of four bases
(Llama-3.1, Qwen, Yi), with TIES best on Mistral; TA is the \emph{worst}
method on every base by a factor of $2$--$100\times$. The R3 ordering
``TIES or TVQ-$b{=}2$, not TA'' therefore holds on both NLL and
pass@1.

\paragraph{R4 --- Verify on $\geq 2$ downstream accuracy
metrics before deploying.}
Across $4 \text{ bases} \times 2 \text{ metrics} = 8$
(base $\times$ metric) cells we measured, NLL ranking predicts
accuracy ranking with Spearman $\rho \geq +0.72$ on $7$ cells
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-downstream-gsm8k}, Table~\ref{tab:b4-humaneval}).
The single outlier is (Llama-3.1-Instruct, GSM8K \textsc{em})
at $\rho = -0.60$; the same matrix on HumanEval pass@1
shows $\rho = +0.89$ on Llama-3.1, so the inversion does not
generalize across metrics. Both pre-registered mechanism
candidates for the outlier (H1 chat-template emission, H3
sign-election cross-metric contamination) were tested and
empirically falsified. The practical guidance: run at least
two downstream metrics on at least one representative task
before deployment --- the L3-GSM8K cell shows that
single-metric verification can mislead, but the cross-metric
agreement on $7$ of $8$ cells we measured is strong evidence
that NLL is a reliable proxy when corroborated by a second
metric.

\paragraph{R5 --- Estimate the floor before assuming you are
algorithm-bound.}
Use the recipe of \S\ref{subsec:floor-recipe} on your trained adapter
artifacts to compute $\Phi^\star_{\mathrm{lower}} = B^2 (1 -
d_{\mathrm{eff}} / (Tr))$. If the audited cohort is in the floor-zero
regime ($d_{\mathrm{eff}} = Tr$) --- as every cohort in our
measurements is --- the floor vanishes and the entire measured
worst-task excess is recoverable algorithmic slack; rd-encoder ridge
from TA is the highest-yield change available. If the cohort has
$d_{\mathrm{eff}} < Tr$ the floor is strictly positive and bounds the
smallest excess any encoder at any rate can achieve; the gap
$\Phi^\star_{\mathrm{meas}} - \Phi^\star_{\mathrm{lower}}$ is the
algorithmic gap, and only the gap is recoverable. We have not yet
seen a real-adapter cohort with $d_{\mathrm{eff}} < Tr$; the
floor-positive regime (\S\ref{subsec:exp-e5-null}) requires
$r > d_{\mathrm{in}} / T$, which lies outside the practical LoRA rank
range for current
LLMs.

\paragraph{What we deliberately do not recommend.}
We do not recommend per-adapter random subspace selection (DARE)
without TA fallback when sign-coherence is uncertain; on our matrix
DARE tracks TA to three decimals (\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed} C3),
so the random projection step does not add value over TA on the
$T = 4$ matrix. We also do not recommend KnOTS for new adapter
cohorts unless the joint orthogonal alignment is known to be cheap
to compute on the specific architecture; its empirical performance
on our $T = 4$ matrix is indistinguishable from TA, and at $T = 7$
on Llama it tracks TA exactly ($0.265$ vs $0.265$). Both methods
remain useful baselines for the algorithmic comparison but do not
offer practical advantages over TA in the cohorts we tested.
