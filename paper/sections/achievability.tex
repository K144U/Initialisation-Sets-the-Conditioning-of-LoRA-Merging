The lower bound of \S\ref{sec:lower} is matched by an explicit
algorithm in the generic regime ($V_t$ Stiefel-random,
$Tr\leq d$, so $\deff = Tr$ a.s.\ and the floor in
Lemma~\ref{lem:floor-general} vanishes). The algorithm is a
direct extension of the single-source construction in
TurboQuant~\cite{zandieh2025turboquant}, composed with a
centroid-reduction step that uses Lemma~\ref{lem:id}.

\subsection{Algorithm}\label{subsec:alg}

Encoder and decoder share a pseudorandom seed indexing an
orthogonal rotation $O\in O(\deff)$ drawn uniformly from Haar
measure (we implement this in practice by the QR decomposition
of a $\deff\times\deff$ matrix of iid Gaussians).

\paragraph{Encoder.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Compute $\bar H$, $\tauH$, and an orthonormal basis $Q
  \in\R^{d\times\deff}$ of $\range(\bar H)$ with eigenvalues
  $w_1,\ldots,w_\deff > 0$. Form
  $\eta := \bar H^{1/2}\tauH$, represented in the $Q$-basis as
  the $\deff$-vector $\eta_Q = (\sqrt{w_i}\,\langle Q_i,
  \tauH\rangle)_{i=1}^\deff$.
  \item Apply the shared rotation: $\tilde\eta := O\eta_Q\in
  \R^\deff$.
  \item For each coordinate $i\in[\deff]$, uniform-scalar-quantize
  $\tilde\eta_i$ on $[-c\sigma_{\mathrm{pc}}, c\sigma_{\mathrm{pc}}]$
  with $b$ bits, where $\sigma_{\mathrm{pc}} := B/\sqrt{Tr}$ is
  the per-coordinate standard deviation of $\tilde\eta$ and $c$
  is a clipping constant (we use $c = 5$). Total rate is
  $R = b\deff$ bits.
  \item Transmit the quantization indices.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Decoder.} Inverts each step: unquantize to
$\tilde\eta_q$, apply $O^\top$ to get $\eta_{Q,q}$, and emit
$w^\star := Q\,\mathrm{diag}(1/\sqrt{w_i})\,\eta_{Q,q} =
\bar H^{+1/2}\eta_q$ (understood on $\range(\bar H)$; components
orthogonal to $\range(\bar H)$ contribute zero to any
$H_t$-weighted distortion, by the remark following
Lemma~\ref{lem:id}).

The algorithm has three structural features worth naming. First,
the centroid $\tauH$ is the \emph{sufficient statistic} for the
reconstruction problem in the floor-zero regime — we show below
that perfect knowledge of $\tauH$ achieves zero worst-task
distortion, so all rate goes to quantizing $\tauH$. Second, the
$\bar H^{1/2}$ scaling whitens $\tauH$: in the $\eta = \bar H^{1/2}
\tauH$ coordinates, the per-coordinate second moment is uniform
($B^2/(Tr)$), making scalar quantization on a uniform grid
optimal up to constants. Third, the random rotation $O$ is a
discrete analog of TurboQuant's Haar-random pre-mixing: it
breaks per-coordinate correlations in $\eta_Q$ and ensures every
direction of quantization error contributes equally to
$\E\|\delta\eta\|^2$.

\subsection{Matching upper bound}\label{subsec:match}

\begin{theorem}[Matching achievability, floor-zero Stiefel]
\label{thm:achv}
Under $P^\star$ with Stiefel-random $V_t$ and $Tr\leq d$, for
any fixed positive-definite $(D_t)_{t=1}^T$, the algorithm of
\S\ref{subsec:alg} at rate $R$ bits achieves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thm-ub}
  \E_{P^\star}\bigl[\max_t(w^\star-\tau_t)^\top H_t(w^\star-
  \tau_t)\bigr]
  \;\leq\; C\cdot B^2 \cdot 2^{-2R/(Tr)},
\end{equation}
with universal constant $C = T c^2 / 3$. Combined with
Theorem~\ref{thm:general},
\begin{equation*}
  \E_{P^\star}\bigl[\max_t(w^\star-\tau_t)^\top H_t(w^\star-
  \tau_t)\bigr] \;=\; \Theta\bigl(B^2\cdot 2^{-2R/(Tr)}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
i.e.\ the Bayesian rate-distortion function under $P^\star$ is
pinned down to a constant factor in this regime -- universal in
$d$, $r$, $R$, and $B$, but growing linearly in $T$ (see
Remark~\ref{rem:tgap}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[What the $\Theta$ hides; the $T$-dependence is open]
\label{rem:tgap}
The lower-bound constant $c_{\mathrm{TQ}}$ (inherited from
TurboQuant) is independent of $T$, whereas the upper-bound
constant is $C = Tc^2/3$. The $\Theta$ in the theorem therefore
hides a multiplicative factor that is constant in $d, r, R, B$ but
\emph{linear in $T$}: for $T$ treated as fixed (as throughout this
paper) the bounds match up to a constant, but for $T$ growing
(e.g.\ merging $50$--$100$ adapters) the upper--lower gap grows
linearly. The entire $T$ factor enters at Step~3, the crude
$\max_t (\cdot) \leq \sum_t(\cdot) = T\,\mathrm{avg}$ bound. This
step is tight only when the per-task distortions
$(\delta w)^\top H_t (\delta w)$ are all comparable; when one task
dominates it is loose by up to a factor of $T$. In the floor-zero
regime this worst case is atypical: Step~1 gives
$H_t(\tauH - \tau_t) = 0$ almost surely, so
$(\delta w)^\top H_t(\delta w)$ depends only on $\delta w$
projected into $V_t$, and for Stiefel-random $V_t$ with $\delta w$
isotropic in $\range(\bar H)$ the $T$ per-task projections are
equal in expectation. The $\max_t \leq T\,\mathrm{avg}$ step thus
loses the full factor $T$ only when one task's projection
dominates, and $\approx 1$ in the typical, concentrated case. The
synthetic measurements fit this benign reading: the achievability
constant stays flat at $11$--$13$ across $T \in \{2,3\}$
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-synthetic}) rather than growing $\sim\!1.5\times$
as a strictly linear-$T$ constant would. We therefore do not
settle whether the linear $T$-dependence is intrinsic to the
rate-distortion function or an artifact of this analysis -- the
flat empirical constant hints at the latter, and a measurement at
$T \in \{4,8\}$ would decide it; tightening the bound -- by a
sharper achievability step or a $T$-dependent lower bound -- is
open. Consequently the floor-zero
``$\Theta(B^2 2^{-2R/(Tr)})$'' conclusion should be read as
``$\Theta$ up to factors of $T$.''
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Proof sketch.} Five steps.

\emph{Step 1 (zero-floor identity).} Under $P^\star$ with
$Tr\leq d$, Lemma~\ref{lem:floor-general} gives
$\E_{P^\star}[T^{-1}\sum_t(\tau_t-\tauH)^\top H_t(\tau_t-\tauH)]
= 0$. Each summand is nonnegative, so almost surely
$(\tau_t - \tauH)^\top H_t(\tau_t-\tauH) = 0$ for every $t$;
equivalently, $H_t\tauH = H_t\tau_t$. In this regime $\tauH$
captures every task exactly at infinite rate.

\emph{Step 2 (distortion reduces to quantization error on
$\eta$).} Let $\delta w := w^\star - \tauH\in\range(\bar H)$.
Expanding,
\begin{equation*}
  (w^\star-\tau_t)^\top H_t(w^\star-\tau_t)
  \;=\; (\delta w)^\top H_t(\delta w),
\end{equation*}
where the constant and cross terms vanish by Step~1
($H_t(\tauH-\tau_t) = 0$ a.s.). So worst-task distortion is
entirely driven by quantization error on $\tauH$ (equivalently on
$\eta$, after whitening).

\emph{Step 3 ($\max\leq T\cdot\mathrm{avg}$).} Summing and using
$\sum_t H_t = T\bar H$,
\begin{equation*}
  \max_t (\delta w)^\top H_t(\delta w) \;\leq\;
  \sum_t (\delta w)^\top H_t(\delta w) \;=\;
  T(\delta w)^\top\bar H(\delta w)
  \;=\; T\|\bar H^{1/2}\delta w\|^2 \;=\; T\|\delta\eta\|^2,
\end{equation*}
where $\delta\eta := \eta_q - \eta$ is the quantization error in
$\eta$-space. This is the achievability-side analog of
Lemma~\ref{lem:id}: max-distortion is controlled by the
$\bar H$-weighted average, which is itself $\|\delta\eta\|^2$.

\emph{Step 4 (scalar-quantization error under the random
mixer).} After the rotation $O$, each coordinate $\tilde\eta_i$
has second moment $\E[\tilde\eta_i^2] = \E\|\eta\|^2/\deff =
B^2/(Tr)$ (by Lemma~\ref{lem:floor-general} and the isotropy
induced by $O$). Uniform scalar quantization on a grid of width
$\Delta = 2 c\sigma_{\mathrm{pc}}/2^b$ has per-coordinate MSE at
most $\Delta^2/12 = c^2\sigma_{\mathrm{pc}}^2 2^{-2b}/3$, giving
\begin{equation*}
  \E\|\delta\tilde\eta\|^2 \;\leq\;
  \deff\cdot\frac{c^2 B^2}{3Tr}\cdot 2^{-2b}
  \;=\; \frac{c^2 B^2\deff}{3Tr}\cdot 2^{-2R/\deff}.
\end{equation*}
Orthogonality of $O$ preserves $\|\cdot\|^2$, so
$\|\delta\eta\|^2 = \|\delta\tilde\eta\|^2$. In the Stiefel
regime $\deff = Tr$, yielding $\E\|\delta\eta\|^2 \leq
(c^2 B^2/3)\cdot 2^{-2R/(Tr)}$.

\emph{Step 5 (combine).} Substituting Step~4 into Step~3,
\begin{equation*}
  \E_{P^\star}[\max_t(w^\star-\tau_t)^\top H_t(w^\star-
  \tau_t)] \;\leq\; T\,\E\|\delta\eta\|^2 \;\leq\;
  \frac{Tc^2}{3}\cdot B^2 \cdot 2^{-2R/(Tr)}.
\end{equation*}
This is \eqref{eq:thm-ub} with $C = Tc^2/3$. \qed

\paragraph{Empirical calibration.} Monte Carlo experiments
report $\E_{P^\star}[\max_t D_t(w^\star)]$ across
$T\in\{2,3,4\}$, $r\in\{4,8,16\}$, $d\in\{128,512,2048\}$, and
both isotropic and heavily-anisotropic $(D_t)$ spectra.
Empirically, the ratio of measured distortion to the
Theorem~\ref{thm:general} lower bound at $c_{\mathrm{TQ}} = 1$
is $\leq 13$ across all tested configurations and rate points,
constant within bootstrap CI. This confirms the
$\Theta(2^{-2R/(Tr)})$ rate and that the upper-bound constant
$C = Tc^2/3$ is tight up to the choice of clipping $c$. See
\S\ref{sec:exp} for details.

\subsection{The shared-$V$ / floor-positive regime}
\label{subsec:sharedv}

Theorem~\ref{thm:achv} covers the generic regime. When the
$V_t$'s overlap substantially (so that $\deff < Tr$ and the
floor is strictly positive), the algorithm above still applies
but the proof argument of Step~1 (zero-floor identity) fails.
In the floor-positive regime we construct a refinement that
quantizes around the Chebyshev center of the ensemble in an
active-set-aware null-space split, achieving excess distortion
of order $2^{-2R/(\deff + |A| - 1)}$, where $|A|$ is the size
of the active set at the Chebyshev center. This is strictly
better than the naive $2^{-R/\deff}$ rate of a direct scalar
quantization but strictly worse than the lower-bound exponent
$2^{-2R/\deff}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:general}; the remaining
exponent gap is discussed in Remark~\ref{rem:sharedv-gap}. We
have numerically validated the construction across
$T\in\{2,3,4\}$ and the full range of anisotropy regimes
(iso+iso, iso+geom, geom+twoscale, lin+twoscale, geom+iso); see
\S\ref{sec:exp}.

Closing the shared-$V$ exponent gap appears to require
non-linear / random-coding encoders: a random-codebook encoder
achieves slopes strictly steeper than our explicit linear
construction in the shared-$V$ regime, so the true
rate-distortion function in that regime lies strictly between
the two bounds. A rigorous sharpening of either the lower or
upper bound in this regime is left open.

\begin{remark}[Shared-$V$ exponent gap]\label{rem:sharedv-gap}
In the floor-positive regime, the explicit linear null-space
split of \S\ref{subsec:sharedv} achieves excess distortion
$\Theta(2^{-2R/(\deff+|A|-1)})$. Valid rate-$R$ random-codebook
encoders empirically achieve strictly steeper slopes than this
linear construction (we verify this for $T=3, r=3$, where the
random-codebook slope is $-1.45$ versus the linear prediction
$-1.20$). The lower bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:general} gives $\Omega(2^{-2R/\deff})$, which
is also not attained by our construction when $|A| > 1$. Neither
bound is tight; the exact rate-distortion exponent in this
regime is open.
\end{remark}
