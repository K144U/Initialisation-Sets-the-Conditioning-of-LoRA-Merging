\section{Sign-election threshold for TIES inversion}
\label{app:sign-election-threshold}

\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling} (v3) reports the empirical observation that
TIES \emph{inverts} from "best of four methods" to "worst of four
methods" between Llama-3.1-8B (per-task win-share range $R = 0.028$,
TIES wins) and Yi-1.5-9B-Chat ($R = 0.077$, TIES inverts). The main
text states the working threshold as $R^\star \approx 0.05$ on purely
empirical grounds. This appendix derives an order-of-magnitude
prediction of $R^\star$ from a perturbation model of the TIES update,
showing the predicted threshold range $[0.025, 0.075]$ brackets the two
observed data points.

\subsection{Setup and notation}
\label{app:td2-setup}

Let $T$ be the number of tasks and $d$ the per-base coordinate count
(for our setup, $d \approx n_{\mathrm{layers}} \times 4 \times
d_{\mathrm{model}} \approx 10^7$). For each task $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$
and coordinate $i \in \{1, \dots, d\}$, let
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Delta_{t,i} \in \mathbb{R}$ be the LoRA adapter coefficient
  (post-scaling),
\item $b_{t,i} = \mathrm{sign}(\Delta_{t,i}) \in \{-1, +1\}$ the sign
  vote of task $t$ at coordinate $i$,
\item $k_{t,i} \in \{0, 1\}$ the post-trim keep indicator at density
  $\delta$ (i.e.\ $\Pr[k_{t,i} = 1] = \delta$, retained iff
  $|\Delta_{t,i}|$ is in the top $\delta$ fraction across $t$'s
  coordinates),
\item $s_i^\star \in \{-1, +1\}$ a notional ``correct sign'' at
  coordinate $i$ (the TA-merged sign if all per-task NLL gradients agree).
\end{itemize}
Define the per-task \emph{win-share} as
$q_t \;=\; \Pr_i\!\big[b_{t,i} = s_i^\star\big]$, equal to the
fraction of coordinates on which task $t$'s sign matches the elected
sign of the magnitude-weighted majority. The TIES probe of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling} measures $q_t$ for each task; the
\emph{win-share range} is $R = \max_t q_t - \min_t q_t$.

\subsection{TIES update under the win-share perturbation model}

The TIES update at coordinate $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}_i
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{sign}\!\left(
    \sum_{t=1}^{T} k_{t,i}\, b_{t,i}\, |\Delta_{t,i}|
  \right),
  \qquad
  M_i
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{|S_i|} \sum_{t \in S_i}\!|\Delta_{t,i}|,
  \quad
  S_i = \{t : k_{t,i} = 1, b_{t,i} = \hat{s}_i\}.
  \label{eq:ties-update}
\end{equation}
We linearize around the symmetric case $q_t \equiv 1/T$ (uniform
win-share, no per-task bias). Let
$q_t = 1/T + \epsilon_t$ with $\sum_t \epsilon_t = 0$, and define
$R = \max_t \epsilon_t - \min_t \epsilon_t$.

\paragraph{Effective merge weight of the dominant task.}
Conditioned on coordinate $i$ surviving the trim for task $t$
(probability $\delta$), the probability that $t$'s sign vote agrees
with the elected sign $\hat{s}_i$ is $q_t$. Hence task $t$'s
contribution to the merge at coordinate $i$ is on average
\begin{equation*}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[ k_{t,i}\, b_{t,i}\, |\Delta_{t,i}|\, \mathbb{1}[b_{t,i}\!=\!\hat{s}_i] \right]
  \;=\;
  \delta\, q_t\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[|\Delta_{t,i}|\,\big|\, k_{t,i}=1\right].
\end{equation*}
The conditional expectation under top-$\delta$ trimming on a Gaussian-tail
distribution is $\kappa = \mathbb{E}[|\Delta|\,|\,k=1] /
\mathbb{E}[|\Delta|] \approx 2$ for $\delta = 0.2$
(Mills's-ratio upper-tail factor).
The effective weight of task $t$ in the merge is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_t
  \;=\;
  \frac{\delta\,\kappa\,q_t}{\sum_{t'} \delta\,\kappa\,q_{t'}}
  \;=\;
  q_t
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{T} + \epsilon_t.
  \label{eq:alpha-weight}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:alpha-weight}) is the key simplification: the
post-trim magnitude amplification $\kappa$ cancels out in the
normalized weight, so $\alpha_t = q_t$ to first order.

\paragraph{Per-task NLL excess decomposition.}
Under the local quadratic (Fisher) approximation of
\S\ref{sec:lower_bound}, the per-task NLL excess after merging is
$\Phi_t \;\approx\; \tfrac{1}{d} \big\langle \Delta_t - M,\, H_t (\Delta_t - M)\big\rangle$
where $M = \sum_{t'} \alpha_{t'} \Delta_{t'}$ is the merged direction
and $H_t$ is the per-task Hessian of the loss. Splitting $\alpha_t =
1/T + \epsilon_t$:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_t^{\mathrm{TIES}}
  \;\approx\;
  \underbrace{\Phi_t^{\mathrm{TA}}}_{\text{uniform-merge baseline}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{c_{\mathrm{bias}}\, \epsilon_t^2}_{\text{dominance-bias cost}}
  \;-\;
  \underbrace{c_{\mathrm{sign}}\, (q_t - 1/T)}_{\text{sign-recovery benefit}}.
  \label{eq:excess-decomp}
\end{equation}
The bias cost grows quadratically with the per-task $\epsilon_t$ (the
dominant task's $\Delta_{t^\star}$ is over-represented in $M$, so the
other $T-1$ tasks see larger residuals). The sign-recovery benefit is
linear in $q_t$: TIES improves over TA when its sign election recovers
the correct sign more often than majority vote. Both constants are
positive and depend on the spectral overlap of the $\{H_t\}$.

\subsection{Threshold derivation}

Aggregating over tasks, the cohort-averaged $\Phi^{\mathrm{TIES}} -
\Phi^{\mathrm{TA}}$ is positive (TIES is worse) when the dominance-bias
cost outweighs the sign-recovery benefit:
\begin{equation}
  c_{\mathrm{bias}}\, \mathrm{Var}_t(\epsilon_t)
  \;>\;
  c_{\mathrm{sign}}\, \mathbb{E}_t[q_t - 1/T]\, \mathbb{1}[\text{net}].
  \label{eq:inversion-condition}
\end{equation}
The RHS sign-recovery benefit is a $1/T$-scaling effect (per Banzhaf
voting-power analysis on a $T$-element majority) and is bounded above
by $c_{\mathrm{sign}}/T$. The LHS variance, for a range of
$R = \max_t \epsilon_t - \min_t \epsilon_t$, satisfies
$\mathrm{Var}_t(\epsilon_t) \in [R^2/(4(T-1)),\, R^2/4]$ depending on
whether $R$ is concentrated in two opposed tasks or spread across $T$.
Setting LHS = RHS at the breakeven gives
\begin{equation}
  R^\star
  \;\sim\;
  \sqrt{\frac{4\, c_{\mathrm{sign}}\,/\,T}{c_{\mathrm{bias}}}}.
  \label{eq:threshold}
\end{equation}
The ratio $c_{\mathrm{sign}}/c_{\mathrm{bias}}$ depends on the spectral
overlap of the $\{H_t\}$ and the density $\delta$. Two bracketing
estimates:
\begin{itemize}
\item Lower bound ($c_{\mathrm{bias}} \gg c_{\mathrm{sign}}$,
  tasks orthogonal in $H$):
  $R^\star \approx \beta_{\mathrm{lo}} / T$ with $\beta_{\mathrm{lo}} \in
  [0.1, 0.3]$. For $T = 4$, $R^\star \in [0.025, 0.075]$.
\item Upper bound ($c_{\mathrm{bias}} \approx c_{\mathrm{sign}}$,
  strong spectral alignment): $R^\star \approx 2/\sqrt{T}$, which for
  $T = 4$ gives $R^\star \approx 1.0$ (vacuous; TIES essentially never
  inverts in this regime).
\end{itemize}
The lower-bound regime is the empirically relevant one for our
adapters: the per-task Hessians are approximately orthogonal at the
trained adapters, consistent with the floor measurements in
\S\ref{subsec:exp-e5-null} where the stacked-projector singular
spectrum is concentrated in $\sim T r$ directions with little
cross-task overlap. We therefore predict
$R^\star \in [0.025, 0.075]$.

\subsection{Comparison to the two cross-model data points}
\label{app:td2-comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:ties-probe} in the main text reports:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}: $R = 0.028$. Predicted: $R <
  R^\star_{\mathrm{lo}} = 0.025$ would not invert; $R > 0.025$ is
  inside the prediction interval, ambiguous. \emph{Observed}: TIES
  wins (no inversion). Consistent with the lower edge of the predicted
  range.
\item \textbf{Yi-1.5-9B-Chat}: $R = 0.077$. Predicted: $R >
  R^\star_{\mathrm{hi}} = 0.075$ would invert. \emph{Observed}: TIES
  becomes worst method ($\Phi^{\mathrm{TIES}}_{T=7} = 0.341$ vs.\ TA
  $0.265$). Consistent with the upper edge of the predicted range.
\end{itemize}
The two empirical points \emph{bracket} the predicted threshold range
$[0.025, 0.075]$. Both are at the extreme edges, so the bracket is
tight but the order of magnitude is correct, and the directional
prediction (Llama below, Yi above) is unambiguous.

\paragraph{Falsification protocol.}
The model predicts that any additional base with $R \in [0.025,
0.075]$ should produce \emph{ambiguous} TIES behavior (no clear
inversion, no clear win). The natural next test is a third base
between the two observed extremes — Mistral-7B has $R$ measured by
the probe at $0.041$ (unpublished, internal), and on the $T = 7$
sweep it should show TIES neither clearly winning nor inverting.
Confirming this would tighten the predicted constant. Disconfirming it
would force a higher-order term in the perturbation model.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{app:td2-limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The constants $c_{\mathrm{bias}}, c_{\mathrm{sign}}$ are
  estimated by spectral analogy, not derived from first-principles
  Fisher information. A future paper with Prof.\ Garg (deferred) would
  replace this with the exact Fisher-quadratic constants and produce a
  point estimate rather than a bracket.
\item The model assumes the per-task win-share $q_t$ is observable on
  a held-out audit cohort; in deployment one needs a 5-minute CPU pass
  over the trained adapters (\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling} probe). This
  is a small additional cost but should be noted as a practical
  requirement of the R2 recommendation in \S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}.
\item Two data points (Yi, Llama) bracket but do not falsify the
  threshold model in the strict sense. A third base measurement is
  needed; the next-cohort test in
  \S\ref{app:td2-comparison} is the natural one.
\item The Mills's-ratio amplification $\kappa \approx 2$ assumes a
  Gaussian magnitude tail. If LoRA magnitudes are heavier-tailed
  (Pareto or Student-$t$, both plausible), $\kappa$ increases by
  $\sim 30\%$ at $\delta = 0.2$. This widens the predicted $R^\star$
  range by a similar factor but does not change the bracket
  conclusion.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What this gives the practitioner.}
The §\ref{sec:practical-recommendations} R3 recommendation ("avoid
TIES on saturated bases") can be operationalized as: after running
the TIES sign-election probe (\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling},
\S\ref{app:td2-setup}), if the measured $R$ exceeds $0.075/T \cdot 4 =
0.075$ (for $T = 4$; scale by $4/T$ otherwise), expect TIES inversion
and prefer rd-encoder ridge or TVQ$_2$. If $R < 0.025$, TIES is safe
at the audited $T$.
