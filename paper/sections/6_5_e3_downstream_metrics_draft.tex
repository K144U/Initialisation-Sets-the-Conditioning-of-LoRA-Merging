\subsection{Do NLL conclusions hold on task accuracy?}
\label{subsec:exp-downstream-gsm8k}

The negative-log-likelihood per response token of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}--\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder} is fast to
compute and seed-stable, but it is not the metric a practitioner
deploys against. We verify here that the NLL-based method
comparisons survive on an accuracy metric. We focus on GSM8K
exact-match (\textsc{em}) as the cleanest first cut --- the gold
answer is a single number, extraction is unambiguous, and the
seed-0 NLL ordering matches the worst-task ordering across methods.

\paragraph{Setup.} For each of the four base models, we re-merged
the four task adapters of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} using the five
methods that span the matrix structure: Task Arithmetic (TA), TIES,
DARE, KnOTS, and TVQ at $b=2$ (the dip champion of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}). Each merged checkpoint generated
answers to the first $n=500$ GSM8K test prompts with greedy decoding
(max $256$ new tokens, $0$-shot CoT prompt matching the per-task
training format), and we extracted the final numeric answer via
a four-pattern regex (\verb|#### N|, ``answer is $N$'', trailing
equation, or last number) that succeeded in $95\%$ of generations
in a $n=100$ pilot. \textsc{em} score is the fraction of extracted
predictions matching the gold value to floating-point equality.

\paragraph{Headline: NLL conclusions hold on 3 of 4 bases. Llama-3.1
is the lone outlier.}
Table~\ref{tab:e3-gsm8k} reports the $4 \times 5$ accuracy matrix
alongside the matching worst-task NLL excess from
\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}. On Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and
Yi-1.5-9B, the per-method accuracy ranking agrees strongly with
the NLL ranking (Spearman $\rho \in \{+0.90, +0.90, +0.87\}$),
all above the pre-registered $0.7$ threshold. The best method by
NLL (TIES) is the best method by accuracy on all three. On
Llama-3.1-8B, $\rho = -0.60$: an outright rank inversion. The
methods that minimize NLL excess (TIES and TVQ $b=2$) are the
methods that drop the most accuracy.

\paragraph{The Llama-3.1 anomaly is not explained by raw GSM8K
capacity.}
A natural first hypothesis --- the strongest base is hardest to
merge cleanly --- does not survive scrutiny. Llama-3.1-8B is
\emph{not} the strongest GSM8K base by per-adapter NLL
(Table~\ref{tab:nll-tau}): Yi-1.5-9B has the lowest per-adapter
NLL on GSM8K ($\tau_{\text{gsm8k}} = 0.387$ vs.\ Llama's $0.466$),
and on \textsc{em} accuracy Yi (best of merged: $0.778$) reaches
the highest of any merged checkpoint we tested. Despite being
``stronger'' than Llama by both NLL and accuracy on the solo
checkpoint, Yi sits comfortably with Mistral and Qwen at
$\rho = +0.87$, while Llama inverts. The anomaly is therefore
\emph{model-specific to Llama-3.1-8B}, not a function of task
capacity.

\paragraph{Candidate explanations (open).}
Three concrete and testable explanations remain on the table:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Tokenizer / chat-template idiosyncrasies.} The
Llama-3 family introduces several special tokens in its chat
template (\verb|<|begin_of_text|>|,
\verb|<|start_header_id|>|, \verb|<|end_header_id|>|,
\verb|<|eot_id|>|) that the merged-model state may emit
unpredictably under aggressive trim/quantize compression.
Mistral, Qwen, and Yi use simpler tokenizer additions in their
chat templates and would be less sensitive to this failure mode.
\item \emph{Adapter $\Delta$ magnitude distribution.}
Llama-3.1's instruction-tuned starting point may produce
LoRA $\Delta_t$ tensors with a flatter magnitude distribution
than the other bases (we did not measure this). A flatter
distribution dilutes TIES's top-$0.2$ magnitude threshold
(many coordinates near the threshold are stochastically
included/excluded) and shrinks TVQ's per-tensor $\min/\max$
range relative to per-coordinate magnitudes (so the
$3$-bucket quantization loses more signal per coordinate).
\item \emph{Per-task answer-distribution overlap on the
$\tau$ side.} If Llama-3.1's GSM8K adapter learns reasoning
patterns that overlap heavily with its Alpaca / Magicoder
adapters' formats, TIES's sign election could elect Alpaca's
sign on coordinates that GSM8K \emph{also} wants to use,
discarding GSM8K-specific reasoning. Mistral/Qwen/Yi adapters
may have more distinct per-task answer distributions, leaving
the sign election uncontaminated.
\end{enumerate}
Each is testable with the artifacts we already have on disk;
we leave the disentanglement as a focused mechanism study (
\S\ref{sec:future}).

\paragraph{What this changes about the paper's claims.} Section
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}'s headline ``ridge-encoder beats TA''
result is reported on NLL excess and was not tested on GSM8K
accuracy in this sweep. \emph{Practitioners selecting a merge
method by NLL on Mistral, Qwen, or Yi should make the right
choice; for Llama-3.1 we recommend an accuracy measurement
directly.} The lower-bound theorem and floor-formula results
(\S\ref{sec:lower_bound}, \S\ref{subsec:exp-e5-null}) are
metric-agnostic --- they apply to any well-defined per-task loss
--- and are unaffected.

\paragraph{Hypothesis 2 rejected.}
We tested the Δ-magnitude-distribution-flatness hypothesis directly.
For each $(\text{model}, \text{task})$ v1 adapter (16 pairs total),
we materialized the LoRA $\Delta_t = \mathrm{scaling} \cdot B_t A_t$
per attention projection and measured five distribution shape
statistics on $|\Delta_t|$: $p_{99} / \mathrm{median}$, excess
kurtosis, top-20\% threshold over median, fraction within $\pm 10\%$
of the top-20\% threshold, and coefficient of variation. Aggregated
across the four tasks and all attention projections,
Llama-3.1-8B sits squarely in the middle of the four bases on every
metric: kurtosis $4.98$ versus Mistral $3.94$ (flattest), Qwen $4.64$,
Yi $5.26$ (most peaked); the other four metrics span similar
mid-pack values. Llama-3.1's distribution is neither flatter nor
more peaked than the others. Whatever drives the GSM8K accuracy
inversion, it is not the $\Delta$-magnitude distribution. The two
remaining candidate mechanisms --- L3 chat-template special-token
sensitivity to compression, and TIES sign-election contamination
from per-task answer overlap --- are not directly tested in this
paper and remain open for a focused mechanism study.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
We do not claim NLL is wrong as a methodology. On three of four
bases the NLL ranking predicts the accuracy ranking with high
reliability ($\rho \in \{+0.87, +0.90, +0.90\}$). On the fourth
the practitioner should measure accuracy directly. The empirical
question of whether other base models, other tasks, or other
accuracy metrics expand or contract the set of NLL-trustworthy
configurations is exactly the question E3 is meant to answer;
extending the sweep to HumanEval pass@1, COMET-22, and IFEval
strict is the natural next step. We also do not claim the
Llama-3.1 anomaly is universal across all merge methods or all
quantization budgets --- the dip pattern of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-b2-mechanism} survived the full multi-seed
matrix, so the inversion we report is method-specific in the
methods that compress the most.
