\subsection{Do NLL conclusions hold on task accuracy?}
\label{subsec:exp-downstream-gsm8k}

The negative-log-likelihood per response token of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}--\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder} is fast to
compute and seed-stable, but it is not the metric a practitioner
deploys against. We verify here that the NLL-based method
comparisons (Section~\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}, $b=2$ TVQ dip,
ridge-encoder ordering) survive on an accuracy metric. We focus on
GSM8K exact-match (\textsc{em}) as the cleanest first cut --- the
gold answer is a single number, extraction is unambiguous, and the
seed-0 NLL ranking on GSM8K matches the worst-task ordering across
methods.

\paragraph{Setup.} For each of the four base models, we re-merged
the four task adapters of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} using the five
methods that span the matrix structure: Task Arithmetic (TA), TIES,
DARE, KnOTS, and TVQ at $b=2$ (the dip champion). Each merged
checkpoint generated answers to the first $n=500$ GSM8K test prompts
with greedy decoding (max $256$ new tokens, $0$-shot CoT prompt
matching the per-task training format), and we extracted the final
numeric answer via a four-pattern regex (\verb|#### N|, ``answer is
$N$'', trailing equation, or last number) that succeeded in $95\%$
of generations in a $n=100$ pilot. \textsc{em} score is the fraction
of extracted predictions matching the gold value to floating-point
equality.

\paragraph{Result: model-dependent NLL$\to$accuracy correspondence.}
Table~\ref{tab:e3-gsm8k} reports the $4 \times 5$ accuracy matrix
alongside the matching worst-task NLL excess from
Table~\ref{tab:exp-lora-full}. The pattern that emerges is sharper
and more interesting than the pre-registered ``NLL holds on
accuracy'' confirmation we expected.

\paragraph{The llama-3.1 inversion.}
On Llama-3.1-8B, the NLL methodology of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed} ranks the methods as
$\text{TVQ}_{b=2}~(0.101) < \text{TIES}~(0.147) < \{\text{TA},
\text{DARE}, \text{KnOTS}\}~(0.213)$ on worst-task excess. On
\textsc{em} accuracy, the ranking is
$\text{KnOTS}~(0.346) > \text{DARE}~(0.330) > \text{TA}~(0.324) >
\text{TVQ}_{b=2}~(0.280) > \text{TIES}~(0.250)$: the two methods
that minimize NLL on llama are the two that hurt accuracy the most.
Mistral and Qwen do not show this: there TIES is best on both
metrics.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
We offer the following hypothesis, which we cannot yet test
rigorously: Llama-3.1-8B has the highest GSM8K solo capacity
of the four base models tested ($\tau_{\text{gsm8k}}$ NLL $0.466$
vs.\ Mistral $0.449$, Qwen $0.442$, Yi $0.387$, but on
\textsc{em} accuracy llama-solo dominates; see
Table~\ref{tab:nll-tau-em}). When the per-task adapter encodes a
substantial reasoning capability, methods that aggressively compress
the merge --- TIES's sign election with threshold trimming, TVQ
quantization to three buckets per layer --- discard
adapter-specific information that NLL on a $1{,}000$-token slice
fails to register but greedy generation does. KnOTS and DARE
preserve more of the underlying adapter signal and thus more of
the reasoning trace. On weaker GSM8K bases the merge is
information-limited by the adapter itself, so the compression
penalty does not surface and NLL-best $=$ accuracy-best as the
methodology assumes.

This is consistent with three other patterns in our data: (i)
the cross-model ordering Llama $>$ Mistral $>$ Qwen $>$ Yi on TA
worst-excess (\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}) is exactly the order of
GSM8K-solo accuracy, suggesting the strongest models are hardest
to merge cleanly; (ii) the $b=2$ TVQ dip is real on NLL
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-b2-mechanism}) but accuracy on llama tells the
opposite story, so the dip is doing something that helps with
NLL but hurts with discrete-answer extraction; (iii) TIES is
the matrix-NLL leader on three of four bases, but its sign election
is precisely the step that throws away small per-task disagreements
that may carry useful reasoning bits.

\paragraph{Consequence for the paper's claims.} Section
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}'s headline ``ridge-encoder beats TA''
result is reported on NLL excess; we have not yet tested it on
accuracy. \emph{Until that test is run, claims of the form ``method
X beats method Y under our regularized achievability'' should be
qualified with ``in NLL terms; for downstream accuracy on
high-reasoning tasks like GSM8K, model-specific re-evaluation is
warranted.''} The lower-bound theorem and floor-formula results
(\S\ref{sec:lower_bound}, \S\ref{subsec:exp-e5-null}) are
metric-agnostic --- they apply to any well-defined per-task loss ---
and are unaffected.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim NLL is wrong as
a methodology. On mistral and qwen the NLL ranking predicts the
accuracy ranking with high reliability ($\rho = +0.90$), and
practitioners selecting a merge method by NLL on those bases will
make the right choice. On llama, the practitioner should
\emph{measure accuracy directly} and not trust NLL as a proxy. The
empirical question of which side of this boundary other base models
fall on is exactly the question E3 is meant to answer; expanding
the sweep to additional task metrics (HumanEval pass@1, COMET-22,
IFEval strict) is the natural next step.
