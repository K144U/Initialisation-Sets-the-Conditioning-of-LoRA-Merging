\subsection{Do NLL conclusions hold on task accuracy?}
\label{subsec:exp-downstream-gsm8k}

The negative-log-likelihood per response token of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}--\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder} is fast to
compute and seed-stable, but it is not the metric a practitioner
deploys against. We verify here that the NLL-based method
comparisons survive on an accuracy metric. We focus on GSM8K
exact-match (\textsc{em}) as the cleanest first cut --- the gold
answer is a single number, extraction is unambiguous, and the
seed-0 NLL ordering matches the worst-task ordering across methods.

\paragraph{Setup.} For each of the four base models, we re-merged
the four task adapters of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} using the five
methods that span the matrix structure: Task Arithmetic (TA), TIES,
DARE, KnOTS, and TVQ at $b=2$ (the dip champion of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}). Each merged checkpoint generated
answers to the first $n=500$ GSM8K test prompts with greedy decoding
(max $256$ new tokens, $0$-shot CoT prompt matching the per-task
training format), and we extracted the final numeric answer via
a four-pattern regex (\verb|#### N|, ``answer is $N$'', trailing
equation, or last number) that succeeded in $95\%$ of generations
in a $n=100$ pilot. \textsc{em} score is the fraction of extracted
predictions matching the gold value to floating-point equality.

\paragraph{Headline: NLL conclusions hold on 3 of 4 bases. Llama-3.1
is the lone outlier.}
Table~\ref{tab:e3-gsm8k} reports the $4 \times 5$ accuracy matrix
alongside the matching worst-task NLL excess from
\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}. On Mistral-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B, and
Yi-1.5-9B, the per-method accuracy ranking agrees strongly with
the NLL ranking (Spearman $\rho \in \{+0.90, +0.90, +0.87\}$),
all above the pre-registered $0.7$ threshold. The best method by
NLL (TIES) is the best method by accuracy on all three. On
Llama-3.1-8B, $\rho = -0.60$: an outright rank inversion. The
methods that minimize NLL excess (TIES and TVQ $b=2$) are the
methods that drop the most accuracy.

\paragraph{The Llama-3.1 anomaly is not explained by raw GSM8K
capacity.}
A natural first hypothesis --- the strongest base is hardest to
merge cleanly --- does not survive scrutiny. Llama-3.1-8B is
\emph{not} the strongest GSM8K base by per-adapter NLL
(Table~\ref{tab:nll-tau}): Yi-1.5-9B has the lowest per-adapter
NLL on GSM8K ($\tau_{\text{gsm8k}} = 0.387$ vs.\ Llama's $0.466$),
and on \textsc{em} accuracy Yi (best of merged: $0.778$) reaches
the highest of any merged checkpoint we tested. Despite being
``stronger'' than Llama by both NLL and accuracy on the solo
checkpoint, Yi sits comfortably with Mistral and Qwen at
$\rho = +0.87$, while Llama inverts. The anomaly is therefore
\emph{model-specific to Llama-3.1-8B}, not a function of task
capacity.

\paragraph{Candidate explanations and what we found.}
Three concrete and testable explanations were on the table; we
tested each.

\textbf{H1 --- Tokenizer / chat-template idiosyncrasies.} The
Llama-3 family introduces several special tokens in its chat
template (\verb|<|begin_of_text|>|,
\verb|<|start_header_id|>|, \verb|<|end_header_id|>|,
\verb|<|eot_id|>|) that the merged-model state may emit
unpredictably under aggressive trim/quantize compression.
We ran a controlled probe: each of the five methods on
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct generated the first $n=100$ GSM8K answers
with \verb|skip_special_tokens=False|, recording the count of
L3 special-token emissions per generation. All five methods
emit exactly $1$ special token per generation on average
($\mathrm{TA}{=}1.00$, $\mathrm{TIES}{=}0.98$,
$\mathrm{DARE}{=}1.00$, $\mathrm{KnOTS}{=}1.00$,
$\mathrm{TVQ}_2{=}0.99$), and $\geq 98\%$ of generations emit
exactly one token (the trailing \verb|<|eot_id|>| that ends each
greedy generation). There is no method-specific chat-template
emission. \textbf{H1 falsified.}

\textbf{H2 --- Adapter $\Delta$ magnitude distribution.} We
tested this directly (see ``Hypothesis 2 rejected'' below).
Llama-3.1's $|\Delta_t|$ distribution sits squarely in the
middle of the four bases on every shape statistic we measured.
\textbf{H2 falsified.}

\textbf{H3 --- Per-task answer-distribution overlap on the
$\tau$ side.} If TIES's sign election were biased on
Llama-3.1-Instruct in a way that hurts deployable accuracy,
the inversion should reproduce on any accuracy metric we test,
not only GSM8K \textsc{em}. We tested this by running the same
$4 \times 5$ matrix on HumanEval pass@1 (greedy CoT, $n = 164$
problems, subprocess sandbox with $5$s timeout per test;
Table~\ref{tab:b4-humaneval}). On every base, including
Llama-3.1-8B, the per-method pass@1 ranking agrees with NLL
ranking at $\rho \geq +0.72$ (Llama-3.1 specifically:
$\rho = +0.894$). The L3 GSM8K $\rho = -0.60$ \emph{does not
replicate} on HumanEval. \textbf{H3 falsified as a
cross-metric mechanism.}

\paragraph{Cross-metric robustness on HumanEval pass@1.}
Table~\ref{tab:b4-humaneval} reports the matching $4 \times 5$
matrix on HumanEval pass@1. The story is
\emph{cleaner} than on GSM8K \textsc{em}: all four bases show
strong NLL$\to$pass@1 agreement, with $\rho$ ranging from
$+0.718$ (Yi) to $+0.975$ (Mistral). TVQ-$b{=}2$ is the
best-pass@1 method on three of four bases (Llama-3.1, Qwen,
Yi); TIES is best on Mistral. Task Arithmetic is the
\emph{worst} method on every base by a factor of $2$--$100\times$:
$\mathrm{TA} = 0.116$ on L3 vs.\ $\mathrm{TVQ}_2 = 0.427$;
$\mathrm{TA} = 0.006$ on Qwen vs.\ $\mathrm{TVQ}_2 = 0.659$.
This is independent confirmation that the methods which
minimize NLL excess also produce more deployable code merges,
and it eliminates the L3 GSM8K inversion as a cross-metric
phenomenon.

\paragraph{What this changes about the paper's claims.} On the
combined $4 \text{ bases} \times 2 \text{ metrics} = 8$
(base $\times$ metric) cells, NLL$\to$accuracy correspondence
holds at $\rho \geq +0.72$ on $7$ cells; the
(Llama-3.1-Instruct, GSM8K \textsc{em}) cell is the single
known outlier. Both candidate mechanisms (H1, H3) for that
outlier were tested directly above and falsified, leaving it
as an open empirical question on a single metric. Section
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}'s headline ``ridge-encoder beats
TA'' result is reported on NLL excess and is consistent with
both downstream metrics on Mistral, Qwen, and Yi.
\emph{Practitioners selecting a merge method by NLL should
make the right choice; we recommend running $\geq 2$
downstream metrics in deployment to detect single-metric
outliers like the L3-GSM8K cell.} The lower-bound theorem and
floor-formula results (\S\ref{sec:lower_bound},
\S\ref{subsec:exp-e5-null}) are metric-agnostic and unaffected.

\paragraph{Bootstrap stability of the cell-level numbers.}
All point estimates in Tables~\ref{tab:e3-gsm8k} and
\ref{tab:b4-humaneval} are reported with $10{,}000$-resample
$95\%$ percentile bootstrap CIs on the per-example scores. The
maximum CI half-width across all $40$ cells is $\pm 0.076$ (the
hardest HumanEval cells; GSM8K cells are tighter at $\pm 0.043$
maximum), which is below the smallest method-to-method gap in any
row whose Spearman correlation drives the headline conclusion. The
single (Llama-3.1-Instruct, GSM8K \textsc{em}) outlier in particular
shows the inversion is bootstrap-robust: TIES at $0.250$
(CI $[0.213, 0.288]$) is significantly below KnOTS at $0.346$
(CI $[0.306, 0.388]$) with non-overlapping CIs.

\paragraph{Hypothesis 2 rejected --- details.}
We tested the $\Delta$-magnitude-distribution-flatness
hypothesis directly. For each $(\text{model}, \text{task})$ v1
adapter (16 pairs total), we materialized the LoRA $\Delta_t =
\mathrm{scaling} \cdot B_t A_t$ per attention projection and
measured five distribution shape statistics on $|\Delta_t|$:
$p_{99} / \mathrm{median}$, excess kurtosis, top-$20\%$
threshold over median, fraction within $\pm 10\%$ of the
top-$20\%$ threshold, and coefficient of variation. Aggregated
across the four tasks and all attention projections,
Llama-3.1-8B sits squarely in the middle of the four bases on
every metric: kurtosis $4.98$ versus Mistral $3.94$ (flattest),
Qwen $4.64$, Yi $5.26$ (most peaked); the other four metrics
span similar mid-pack values. Llama-3.1's distribution is
neither flatter nor more peaked than the others.

\paragraph{Summary.}
The NLL methodology is empirically reliable: on $7$ of $8$
(base $\times$ metric) cells the NLL ranking predicts the accuracy
ranking with high reliability ($\rho \geq +0.72$). On the single
(L3-Instruct, GSM8K \textsc{em}) outlier, both pre-registered
candidate mechanisms (H1 chat-template emission, H3 sign-election
cross-metric contamination) were tested and falsified above, leaving
it as a known generation-evaluation-pipeline curiosity on a single
metric. COMET-22 and IFEval-strict extensions are catalogued as
follow-ups in \S\ref{sec:discussion} alongside the other scope
limitations.
