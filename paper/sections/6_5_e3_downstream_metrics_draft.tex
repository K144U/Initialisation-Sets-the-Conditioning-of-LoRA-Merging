\subsection{Do NLL conclusions hold on task accuracy?}
\label{subsec:exp-downstream-gsm8k}

The negative-log-likelihood per response token of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}--\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder} is fast to
compute and seed-stable, but it is not the metric a practitioner
deploys against. We verify here that the NLL-based method
comparisons survive on an accuracy metric. We focus on GSM8K
exact-match (\textsc{em}) as the cleanest first cut --- the gold
answer is a single number, extraction is unambiguous, and the
seed-0 NLL ordering matches the worst-task ordering across methods.

\paragraph{Setup.} For each of the four base models, we re-merged
the four task adapters of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} using the five
methods that span the matrix structure: Task Arithmetic (TA), TIES,
DARE, KnOTS, and TVQ at $b=2$ (the dip champion of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}), plus our own \textsc{rd-encoder ridge}
at the base-optimal $\lambda$ fixed in \S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}
(so the method is evaluated on accuracy at a $\lambda$ chosen on NLL,
never on the accuracy metric). Each merged checkpoint generated
answers to the first $n=500$ GSM8K test prompts with greedy decoding
(max $256$ new tokens, $0$-shot CoT prompt matching the per-task
training format), and we extracted the final numeric answer via
a four-pattern regex (\verb|#### N|, ``answer is $N$'', trailing
equation, or last number) that succeeded in $95\%$ of generations
in a $n=100$ pilot. \textsc{em} score is the fraction of extracted
predictions matching the gold value to floating-point equality.

\paragraph{Headline: the NLL$\to$accuracy correspondence holds on
$3$ of $4$ bases; Llama-3.1 GSM8K is the lone inversion.}
Table~\ref{tab:e3-gsm8k} reports the $4 \times 5$ baseline accuracy
matrix (plus our rd-ridge column) alongside the matching worst-task
NLL excess from \S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}, all on the matched
seed-$1$/$2$/$3$ adapters ($3$-seed means). On Qwen-2.5-7B and
Yi-1.5-9B the per-method accuracy ranking agrees strongly with the NLL
ranking (Spearman $\rho = +1.00$ and $+0.90$, above the pre-specified
$0.7$ threshold); on Mistral-7B agreement is positive but moderate
($\rho = +0.67$). The best baseline by NLL (TIES) is the best baseline
by accuracy on all three. On Llama-3.1-8B, $\rho = -0.60$: a
point-estimate rank inversion (not individually significant at $n=5$;
see the power note below), where the baselines that minimize NLL excess
(TIES and TVQ $b=2$) are among those that drop the most accuracy.

\paragraph{The Llama-3.1 anomaly is not explained by raw GSM8K
capacity.}
A natural first hypothesis --- the strongest base is hardest to
merge cleanly --- does not survive scrutiny. Llama-3.1-8B is
\emph{not} the strongest GSM8K base by per-adapter NLL
(Table~\ref{tab:nll-tau}): Yi-1.5-9B has the lowest per-adapter
NLL on GSM8K ($\tau_{\text{gsm8k}} = 0.387$ vs.\ Llama's $0.466$),
and on \textsc{em} accuracy Yi (best of merged: $0.778$) reaches
the highest of any merged checkpoint we tested. Despite being
``stronger'' than Llama by both NLL and accuracy on the solo
checkpoint, Yi sits comfortably with Mistral and Qwen at
$\rho = +0.87$, while Llama inverts. The anomaly is therefore
\emph{model-specific to Llama-3.1-8B}, not a function of task
capacity.

\paragraph{Candidate explanations and what we found.}
Three concrete and testable explanations were on the table; we
tested each.

\textbf{H1 --- Tokenizer / chat-template idiosyncrasies.} The
Llama-3 family introduces several special tokens in its chat
template (\verb!<|begin_of_text|>!,
\verb!<|start_header_id|>!, \verb!<|end_header_id|>!,
\verb!<|eot_id|>!) that the merged-model state may emit
unpredictably under aggressive trim/quantize compression.
We ran a controlled probe: each of the five methods on
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct generated the first $n=100$ GSM8K answers
with \verb|skip_special_tokens=False|, recording the count of
L3 special-token emissions per generation. All five methods
emit exactly $1$ special token per generation on average
($\mathrm{TA}{=}1.00$, $\mathrm{TIES}{=}0.98$,
$\mathrm{DARE}{=}1.00$, $\mathrm{KnOTS}{=}1.00$,
$\mathrm{TVQ}_2{=}0.99$), and $\geq 98\%$ of generations emit
exactly one token (the trailing \verb!<|eot_id|>! that ends each
greedy generation). There is no method-specific chat-template
emission. \textbf{H1 falsified.}

\textbf{H2 --- Adapter $\Delta$ magnitude distribution.} We
tested this directly (see ``Hypothesis 2 rejected'' below).
Llama-3.1's $|\Delta_t|$ distribution sits squarely in the
middle of the four bases on every shape statistic we measured.
\textbf{H2 falsified.}

\textbf{H3 --- Per-task answer-distribution overlap on the
$\tau$ side.} If TIES's sign election were biased on
Llama-3.1-Instruct in a way that hurts deployable accuracy,
the inversion should reproduce on any accuracy metric we test,
not only GSM8K \textsc{em}. We tested this by running the same
$4 \times 5$ matrix on HumanEval pass@1 (greedy CoT, $n = 164$
problems, subprocess sandbox with $5$s timeout per test;
Table~\ref{tab:b4-humaneval}). On every base, including
Llama-3.1-8B, the per-method pass@1 ranking agrees with NLL
ranking at $\rho \geq +0.60$ (Llama-3.1 specifically:
$\rho = +1.00$). The L3 GSM8K $\rho = -0.60$ \emph{does not
replicate} on HumanEval. \textbf{H3 falsified as a
cross-metric mechanism.}

\paragraph{Cross-metric robustness on HumanEval pass@1.}
Table~\ref{tab:b4-humaneval} reports the matching $4 \times 5$
matrix on HumanEval pass@1. The key point survives matching: all
four bases show \emph{positive} NLL$\to$pass@1 agreement with no
inversion, $\rho$ ranging from $+0.60$ (Mistral) to $+1.00$
(Llama-3.1). Among the five baselines TVQ-$b{=}2$ is the best-pass@1
method on three of four bases (Llama-3.1, Qwen, Yi) and TIES on
Mistral --- the same ordering as on NLL. The low-density baselines
TA, DARE, and KnOTS cluster at the bottom on every base, a factor of
$2$--$40\times$ below the TVQ/TIES top ($\mathrm{TA} = 0.055$ on L3 vs.\
$\mathrm{TVQ}_2 = 0.441$; $\mathrm{DARE} = 0.014$ on Qwen vs.\
$\mathrm{TVQ}_2 = 0.644$). This confirms that the methods which
minimize NLL excess also produce more deployable code merges,
and it eliminates the L3 GSM8K inversion as a cross-metric
phenomenon.

\paragraph{\textsc{rd-encoder ridge} on accuracy: strong on most
cells, but not a clean sweep.} The method we recommend by NLL
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}) was selected without ever seeing an
accuracy metric; the final column of Tables~\ref{tab:e3-gsm8k} and
\ref{tab:b4-humaneval} reports how it transfers on the matched
seed-$1$/$2$/$3$ adapters. It is the best of all six methods on
\emph{five of the eight} (base$\times$metric) cells --- GSM8K on
Mistral ($0.469$), Qwen ($0.794$), and Yi ($0.791$), and HumanEval on
Llama-3.1 ($0.457$) and Yi ($0.108$); its GSM8K wins on Qwen
($+0.088$ over TIES) and Mistral ($+0.060$) are an order of magnitude
larger than rd-ridge's across-seed SD on those cells ($\leq 0.01$). On
the remaining three cells it does not lead: second on Qwen HumanEval
($0.606$ vs.\ TVQ $0.644$), third on Mistral HumanEval ($0.191$), and
the \emph{worst} of six on Llama-3.1 GSM8K ($0.201$). We do not paper
over the last two. They are precisely the two cells on which rd-ridge
is \emph{seed-unstable}: its per-seed spread is $0.14$--$0.31$ on
Llama-3.1 GSM8K (SD $0.074$) and $0.08$--$0.28$ on Mistral HumanEval
(SD $0.084$), an order of magnitude wider than its SD on the six cells
it wins or ties ($\leq 0.015$). The ridge construction amplifies the
merged-adapter norm (\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}), and on particular
adapter draws that pushes greedy generation into degenerate regions ---
a fragility invisible to the seed-stable NLL it was selected on. This
is a limitation of the \emph{accuracy transfer}, not of the NLL result:
the lowest worst-task NLL excess on all four bases
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}) still holds seed-for-seed. An earlier
draft claimed rd-ridge was best-or-tied on all eight downstream cells;
that was an artifact of single-adapter-draw (``v1'') evaluation, and on
matched three-seed means it is best on five --- we \textbf{retract the
all-eight claim}. The deployment reading is unchanged and in fact
reinforced: select the merge method by the seed-stable NLL, and run
$\geq 2$ downstream metrics to catch the occasional high-variance cell.

\paragraph{What this changes about the paper's claims.} On the
combined $4 \text{ bases} \times 2 \text{ metrics} = 8$
(base $\times$ metric) cells, the baseline NLL$\to$accuracy
correspondence is \emph{positive on $7$} and meets the $\rho \geq 0.7$
threshold on $5$ (Qwen and Yi GSM8K; Llama-3.1, Qwen, and Yi
HumanEval); the two Mistral cells are positive but moderate ($+0.67$,
$+0.60$), and (Llama-3.1-Instruct, GSM8K \textsc{em}) is the single
inversion. Each per-cell $\rho$ is computed across only the five
matrix baselines ($n=5$), so no single cell is individually
conclusive: an exact permutation test gives two-sided $p \approx 0.017$
for the $\rho = 1.00$ cells, $p \approx 0.08$ for $\rho = 0.90$, and
$p \approx 0.35$ for the Llama \textsc{em} inversion ($\rho = -0.60$).
The load-bearing signal is therefore the \emph{across-cell} pattern ---
$7$ of $8$ cells positive, a one-sided sign test at $p \approx 0.035$
--- not any individual coefficient, and we do not read the
$\rho = -0.60$ cell as a robust inversion. We flag that the $8$ cells
are \emph{not} fully independent (the two metrics share bases, merges,
and the underlying NLL values), so this sign test is approximate and
somewhat overstates significance; we therefore lean on it only as
corroboration, not as a formal test. Both candidate mechanisms (H1, H3)
for that outlier were tested directly above and falsified, leaving it
as an open empirical question on a single metric. Recommendation R1
(\S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}) --- select the merge by NLL
worst-task excess --- is earned robustly on NLL (rd-ridge carries the
lowest worst-task excess on all four bases, seed-for-seed) and is the
best accuracy choice on the majority of cells (five of eight), with the
honest caveat that rd-ridge's downstream accuracy is high-variance on
two cells.
\emph{Practitioners selecting a merge method by NLL should
make the right choice on most cells; we recommend running $\geq 2$
downstream metrics in deployment to detect both single-metric
outliers like the L3-GSM8K cell and the occasional high-variance
merge.} The lower-bound theorem and
floor-formula results (\S\ref{sec:lower_bound},
\S\ref{subsec:exp-e5-null}) are metric-agnostic and unaffected.

\paragraph{Stability of the cell-level numbers.}
Every entry in Tables~\ref{tab:e3-gsm8k} and \ref{tab:b4-humaneval} is
a mean over three independently-trained adapter seeds
(seed-$1$/$2$/$3$), which exposes a source of variance a single-seed
bootstrap cannot: the merge's sensitivity to the adapter draw. For the
five matrix baselines the across-seed SD is small ($\lesssim 0.05$ on
every cell we examined, and $\leq 0.025$ on all GSM8K cells),
comfortably below the method-to-method gaps that drive the Spearman
rankings. The exception is \textsc{rd-encoder ridge} on its two losing
cells: across-seed SD $0.074$ on Llama-3.1 GSM8K (per-seed
$0.14$--$0.31$) and $0.084$ on Mistral HumanEval ($0.08$--$0.28$),
versus $\leq 0.015$ on the six cells it wins or ties. This is the
quantitative basis for the retraction above: the single-draw ``v1''
figures for those two cells ($0.364$ and $0.311$) sat near the top of a
wide across-seed range whose mean is much lower. The Llama-3.1 GSM8K
\emph{baseline} inversion is by contrast seed-robust: TIES ($0.256$,
per-seed $0.24$--$0.27$) sits below KnOTS ($0.323$, per-seed
$0.32$--$0.34$) on every seed, so the $\rho = -0.60$ ordering is not a
single-draw artifact.

\paragraph{Hypothesis 2 rejected --- details.}
We tested the $\Delta$-magnitude-distribution-flatness
hypothesis directly. For each $(\text{model}, \text{task})$ v1
adapter (16 pairs total), we materialized the LoRA $\Delta_t =
\mathrm{scaling} \cdot B_t A_t$ per attention projection and
measured five distribution shape statistics on $|\Delta_t|$:
$p_{99} / \mathrm{median}$, excess kurtosis, top-$20\%$
threshold over median, fraction within $\pm 10\%$ of the
top-$20\%$ threshold, and coefficient of variation. Aggregated
across the four tasks and all attention projections,
Llama-3.1-8B sits squarely in the middle of the four bases on
every metric: kurtosis $4.98$ versus Mistral $3.94$ (flattest),
Qwen $4.64$, Yi $5.26$ (most peaked); the other four metrics
span similar mid-pack values. Llama-3.1's distribution is
neither flatter nor more peaked than the others.

\paragraph{Summary.}
The NLL methodology is empirically reliable: on $7$ of $8$
(base $\times$ metric) cells the NLL ranking predicts the accuracy
ranking (positive Spearman), meeting the $\rho \geq 0.7$ threshold on
$5$ and moderate ($+0.60$--$+0.67$) on the two Mistral cells. On the
single (L3-Instruct, GSM8K \textsc{em}) inversion, both pre-specified
candidate mechanisms (H1 chat-template emission, H3 sign-election
cross-metric contamination) were tested and falsified above, leaving
it as a known generation-evaluation-pipeline curiosity on a single
metric. COMET-22 and IFEval-strict extensions are catalogued as
follow-ups in \S\ref{sec:discussion} alongside the other scope
limitations.
