\subsection{Why the floor is operationally zero on real fine-tunes}
\label{subsec:exp-arm2-null}

The lower bound of \S\ref{sec:lower_bound} predicts a non-zero
worst-task distortion floor of $B^2(1 - d_{\mathrm{eff}}/(Tr))$ whenever
the task subspaces overlap ($d_{\mathrm{eff}} < Tr$). On the sixteen
adapters of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} we measured $d_{\mathrm{eff}} = Tr$
in every attention projection for every base model, so the predicted
floor is identically zero. A natural reviewer concern is whether this
is an unlucky choice of task corpora: would deliberately overlapping
fine-tuning data induce $d_{\mathrm{eff}} < Tr$ and bring the floor
into operational range? We test this directly.

\paragraph{Setup.} On Qwen-2.5-7B we trained $4$ task adapters
under three data-mixture levels controlled by a single mixing
parameter $\alpha \in \{0, 0.5, 0.9\}$. At mixing parameter $\alpha$,
the training data for the target task is composed of a fraction
$\alpha$ from a fixed shared pool (uniformly sampled across all four
task corpora, identical across the four adapters) and a fraction
$(1 - \alpha)$ from the target task's own training set. At
$\alpha = 0$ the adapters are independent (and reproduce
\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}); at $\alpha = 0.9$ the four adapters share
$90\%$ of their training data; at $\alpha = 1$ all four adapters
would see identical data and $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ would have to
collapse by counting. This $12$-training pilot is the
pre-registered gate for our floor-positive experiment program: a
GO at $\alpha = 0.9 \Rightarrow d_{\mathrm{eff}}/(Tr) < 0.8$ in a
majority of layers would license the main run.

\paragraph{Outcome: a clean null.} We measured per-layer
hard $d_{\mathrm{eff}}/(Tr)$ across all $112$ attention projections
(Table~\ref{tab:e5-arm2-null}). The hard $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ remains
saturated at $Tr$ for \emph{every} layer at \emph{every} $\alpha$,
including $\alpha = 0.9$. The soft (participation-ratio) variant
drifts down by $2\%$ ($0.257 \to 0.251$ in mean), an amount well
inside the seed-noise scale of \S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}.
$0/112$ layers cross the pre-registered $0.8$ threshold; the gate
fires NO-GO.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Real fine-tuning resists subspace
overlap. The $V_t = \mathrm{topr}\text{-}\mathrm{SVD}(\Delta_t)$ bases
remain linearly independent even when nine in ten training examples
are shared across the four target tasks; the dominant directions
the LoRA updates discover are evidently set by the supervised loss
on the per-task answer distribution, not by the inputs' raw
geometry. The floor formula's $1 - d_{\mathrm{eff}}/(Tr)$ term thus
sits at the boundary of its domain on real adapters in any natural
data regime we have access to.

\paragraph{Consequence for the paper.} The lower bound continues to
apply --- it is a fundamental information-theoretic floor on
worst-task distortion --- but the regime where the floor is
\emph{operationally} non-zero appears not to arise from naive data
overlap. The practical implication is that the
\emph{algorithmic} regime (in which TA, TIES, DARE, etc., all give
positive worst-task excess for purely algorithmic reasons) is the
regime in which all real fine-tunes live, which puts the
regularized achievability construction of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder} squarely on the universally-applicable
side of the bound. We show $d_{\mathrm{eff}} < Tr$ \emph{can} be
forced by reducing $T \cdot r$ relative to layer dimension
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-arm3-geometric}); the floor formula is
non-vacuous under that explicit geometric forcing, exactly as the
theory predicts. Whether a more sophisticated form of overlap
induction (e.g.\ shared output spaces, shared post-attention
projections, multi-task curricula) reaches the floor-positive
regime is left as an open empirical question.
