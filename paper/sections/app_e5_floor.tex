\subsection{Why the floor formula is operationally vacuous on real
LoRA fine-tunes}
\label{subsec:exp-e5-null}

On the sixteen adapters of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} the authors measured
$\deff = Tr$ in every attention projection of every base model, so the
predicted floor $B^2(1 - \deff/(Tr))$ is identically zero. A natural
concern: is this an unlucky choice of task corpora or hyperparameters?
The authors tested two operational routes to break the saturation.

\paragraph{Arm 1: data-mixture overlap.} On Qwen-2.5-7B the authors trained $4$
task adapters under three data-mixture levels: at mixing parameter
$\alpha \in \{0, 0.5, 0.9\}$ the training data for the target task is a
fraction $\alpha$ from a fixed shared pool (uniformly sampled across
all four task corpora) and $(1-\alpha)$ from the target task's own set.
At $\alpha = 0.9$ four adapters share $90\%$ of their training
examples. Per-layer hard $\deff/(Tr)$ stayed saturated at $1.000$
across all $112$ attention projections at every $\alpha$
(Table~\ref{tab:e5-null}); the soft (participation-ratio) statistic
drifted by $2\%$, inside seed noise. \emph{Real fine-tuning resists
subspace overlap}: the top-$r$ right-singular basis of $\Delta_t$ is
set by the per-task answer-distribution loss, not the inputs' raw
geometry, so shared training inputs do not induce shared LoRA
subspaces.

\paragraph{Arm 2: geometric forcing at $r = 64$.} On the same base the authors
trained the four adapters at rank $r = 64$ ($Tr = 256$). Hard
$\deff/(Tr)$: mean $0.997$, min $0.867$, $0/112$ layers below the
pre-specified $0.8$ threshold. The floor formula still evaluates to
zero in the majority of layers and is operationally negligible across
the network.

\paragraph{The mechanical-forcing barrier.} Generically
$\deff = \min(d_{\mathrm{in}}, Tr)$, so $\deff/(Tr) < 1$ requires
$r > d_{\mathrm{in}}/T$: at $T = 4$ this is $r > 1024$ on
Llama-3.1-8B, $r > 896$ on Qwen-2.5-7B, $r > 768$ on Llama-3.2-3B,
all far outside the practical LoRA regime ($r \leq 64$ in nearly all
production deployments). Mechanically forcing the floor-positive
regime would require LoRAs of rank comparable to the hidden dimension,
defeating the parameter-efficiency that motivates LoRA.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The lower bound continues to apply (it
is an information-theoretic floor that always holds) but its
non-vacuous regime sits outside the practical LoRA setting in any
natural way the authors could probe. This is a \emph{positive} finding for the
achievability program: the algorithmic regime, where worst-task excess
is dominated by construction-side suboptimality rather than the
irreducible floor, is the regime all real LoRA fine-tunes live in. The authors
do not rule out a deliberately engineered floor-positive regime (shared
adapter modules, hard-coded subspace constraints, extreme high-rank
LoRA); constructing one is future work.

\subsection{Recipe: estimating the floor on trained adapters}
\label{subsec:floor-recipe}

The recipe below is what the authors used above and what practitioners can
re-run on their own checkpoints; it needs only the adapter artifacts,
not the base model.

\paragraph{Step 1: per-task, per-layer projector.} The effective
dimension is a per-layer quantity. For each attention
projection in each layer $\ell$, form the adapter's column matrix
$M_t^\ell \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}} \times r_t}$ and its rank-$r_t$
orthonormal projector $V_t^\ell = \mathrm{qr}(M_t^\ell).Q$. (A single
global stack across layers would force $\deff = Tr$ trivially and
obscure the per-layer geometry the barrier depends on.)

\paragraph{Step 2: stacked projector and SVD.} Per layer,
concatenate $\mathbf{V}^\ell = [V_1^\ell, \dots, V_T^\ell]$ and take
its singular values $\{\sigma_i\}$. The \emph{hard} per-layer effective
dimension is $d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{hard},\ell} = |\{i : \sigma_i >
10^{-3}\sigma_1\}|$; the \emph{soft} version is the participation ratio
$(\sum_i \sigma_i^2)^2 / \sum_i \sigma_i^4$.

\paragraph{Step 3: per-coordinate radius $B$.} For TVQ at $b$ bits,
$B$ is the $99.9^{\mathrm{th}}$ percentile of $|\Delta_{t,i}|$ across
tasks (the strict max is dominated by 1--2 extreme coordinates); for
continuous methods, the same quantile of $|\Delta_t|$ summed across
tasks.

\paragraph{Step 4: floor and algorithmic gap.}
$\Phi^\star_{\mathrm{lower}} = B^2(1 - \deff/(Tr))$, evaluated per
layer (the Lemma~\ref{lem:floor-general} form). If
$\deff^\ell = Tr$ everywhere (the regime all the authors' measurements occupy),
the floor is identically zero and any measured worst-task excess is
the \emph{entire} algorithmic gap. If $\deff < Tr$, the floor bounds
the smallest excess any encoder at any rate can achieve, and only
$\Phi^\star_{\mathrm{meas}} - \Phi^\star_{\mathrm{lower}}$ is
recoverable.

\paragraph{Caveats.} The adapters must share a base model; the soft
statistic is sensitive to layer-norm scaling (normalize each layer's
$\Delta_t$ to unit Frobenius norm when the scale varies); and the
recipe is a diagnostic floor only; it does not predict downstream
accuracy. A single pass over the adapter directories costs
$\lesssim 1$ CPU-minute per task at $r = 16$.
