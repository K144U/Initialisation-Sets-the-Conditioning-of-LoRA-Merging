Merging several LoRA adapters into one model sometimes costs almost
nothing and sometimes destroys a task outright, and practitioners have
no cheap way to tell which case they are in before they merge. We give
a closed-form lower bound on worst-task negative log-likelihood excess
for rank-$r$ merging, together with a matching achievability
construction. The bound splits into an irreducible floor
$B^2(1 - \deff/(Tr))$, governed by the effective dimension $\deff$ of the
union of the task subspaces, and a rate term decaying as
$2^{-2R/\deff}$.

Applied to real cohorts, the bound points at a property of them that is
not otherwise reported. Whether a cohort's adapters share one
initialisation of the LoRA $A$ factor, a detail almost never recorded,
moves the median principal cosine between task subspaces from $0.047$ to
$0.995$ and the condition number of the merge operator by four to five
orders of magnitude, from $1.5$ to between $1.8\times10^{4}$ and
$8.3\times10^{4}$. The exact interpolator is then $43$ to $64$ times
larger than the mean task delta, against $T$ under independent
initialisation, the orthogonal-subspace value, which the measurement
returns to within $1.4\%$ at both $T=3$ and $T=4$. The whole diagnosis
costs about one CPU minute and needs no forward passes.

We then test, under pre-registration, whether this has operational
consequences, and report the outcome whichever way it fell. It is
invisible to standard merging heuristics: across twenty base-by-method
cells, task arithmetic, TIES, DARE and TVQ are indifferent to which
regime the cohort occupies, because all of them produce bounded-norm,
near-mean solutions that never approach the ill-conditioned direction.
It is severe for methods that solve the linear system: unregularised,
the same merge is $2.1$ to $65$ times worse on the ill-conditioned
cohort, and a single ridge term repairs it. That holds on two solvers,
one of which we did not design, and it grows to $25$ to $117$ times when
the post-merge rank truncation is removed. The mechanism is measured
rather than asserted: the heuristics stay within a factor of two of the
mean task delta with essentially none of their energy in the
ill-conditioned directions, while the unregularised solvers reach $35$ to
$57$ times with three quarters of theirs there.

What we withdraw is as much of this paper as what we claim. The bound's
floor term is \emph{vacuous} for LoRA merging as practised: on every base
model and under every initialisation scheme we tried it is exactly zero,
because $T$ rank-$r$ adapters with $Tr \leq d$ have linearly independent
row spaces even when those spaces are nearly collinear. Our own encoder's
advantage did not survive the move to properly initialised cohorts. A
pre-registered control we wrote ourselves withdrew a headline claim about
benchmark confounding. A rate-exponent falsification we had reported
turned out to rest on a mismatched reference and is withdrawn without
being replaced by a confirmation. And the metric all of this is measured
in does not predict downstream accuracy: pooled over twelve blocks it is
uncorrelated with grade-school mathematics accuracy and \emph{positively}
correlated with code pass rate, which is the wrong sign. What survives is
the bound, the diagnosis, and the audit.
