Merging several LoRA adapters into one model sometimes costs almost
nothing and sometimes destroys a task, and practitioners have no cheap way
to tell which case they are in beforehand. We identify a property of the
adapter cohort that separates the two and that is almost never reported:
whether the $T$ adapters were trained from one shared random draw of the
LoRA $A$ factor, which is what a single global training seed silently
decides. Because $B$ starts at zero, $A$ barely moves during training, so a
shared draw leaves every task with essentially the same row space. Across
four base models this moves the median principal cosine between task
subspaces from $0.047$ to $0.995$: the task directions stop being
distinguishable. Diagnosing which regime a cohort is in costs about one CPU
minute from the adapter weights alone, with no forward passes. We
pre-registered whether the difference matters and report both halves of the
answer. It is invisible to the merging heuristics most people use: across
twenty base-by-method cells, task arithmetic, TIES, DARE and TVQ show no
effect at our $0.01$ nat resolution, because each returns a bounded-norm
near-mean solution that never enters the collapsed directions. It is
decisive for the methods that solve the linear system, which reach $35$ to
$57$ times the mean task delta with three quarters of their energy in
exactly those directions: run unregularised they are $2.1$ to $65$ times
worse on shared-initialisation cohorts, $25$ to $117$ times with the rank
truncation removed, and one Tikhonov term repairs it, on two solvers of
which one is not ours. The route to this result was a rate-distortion lower
bound on worst-task NLL excess, which we prove with a matching achievability
construction and report as provenance rather than as support: its role is to
say which object to measure, its floor term is exactly zero on every real
cohort we tried, and it derives none of the measurements above. Several of
our own claims did not survive pre-registered replication, among them our
encoder's advantage and this metric's link to downstream accuracy, and we
report all of them in full.
