Merging LoRA adapters into a single deployable model is judged
empirically: methods are compared on benchmarks with no notion of how
much room is left to improve, and methods that work on one base
sometimes invert to worst-of-the-matrix on another with no warning. We
attack both problems with a single tool. A worst-task rate-distortion
analysis of LoRA merging produces a closed-form lower bound on
per-coordinate NLL excess, $\Phi^\star \geq B^2 (1 - d_{\mathrm{eff}}
/ (Tr))$ in the data-free quadratic-loss limit, that admits an
exact-construction encoder; on real adapters the exact construction
fails for a measured spectral reason, but a one-line Tikhonov ridge
salvages it into \textsc{rd-encoder ridge} --- a merge method with the
lowest worst-task excess, of all ten methods we benchmark at their
default hyperparameters, on all four bases at $T = 4$ (including the
closest structural relative, data-free RegMean). When the two closest
competitors are themselves tuned by their key hyperparameter (TIES/DARE
density, RegMean ridge $\lambda$), \textsc{rd-encoder ridge} stays best
on three of four bases; on the saturated Yi-1.5-9B-Chat base a tuned
RegMean edges it ($0.033$ vs $0.037$ nats) --- the one base where we
independently flag NLL excess as an unreliable proxy. On a separate
$T$-scaling task pool it holds a lead over Task Arithmetic that widens
with task count (excess ratio $0.84 \to 0.68 \to 0.52$ at
$T = 2, 4, 7$ on Llama-3.1-Instruct). The same analysis yields a $1$-CPU-minute audit
of any trained adapter cohort that diagnoses when classical methods
such as TIES invert from best to worst: a perturbation derivation of
the TIES sign-election update predicts an ambiguity window
$R^\star \in [0.025, 0.075]$ for the per-task win-share range: above
it TIES inverts to worst, inside it TIES is ambiguous. One base above
the window (Yi-Chat, $R = 0.077$) inverts as predicted; two inside it
--- Llama-Instruct ($R = 0.028$) and a pre-registered Mistral-7B-v0.3
($R = 0.0326$) --- are ambiguous as predicted, TIES neither inverting
nor winning (on Mistral, second of six, $0.092$ nats below
Task-Arithmetic). We are deliberately cautious here: the window's
constants were fit to the two in-sample points, the below-window
regime (TIES wins outright) is untested, and the one out-of-sample
test confirmed only the low-information \emph{ambiguous} prediction ---
so we present the threshold as suggestive, not validated. Across
$4 \text{ bases} \times 2 \text{ downstream metrics}$, NLL ranking
predicts accuracy ranking on $7$ of $8$ cells (each Spearman $\rho$ is
over only five methods and individually low-powered; the across-cell
consistency, not any single coefficient, carries the signal). Every cohort we measured sits in the floor-zero regime, so the bound's
floor is identically zero and its rate term falls below sub-bit
resolution on real merges: the theory's empirical role is the
\emph{regime diagnosis} --- none of the $0.10$--$0.22$ nats of
worst-task excess is an information-theoretic floor --- not a numerical
prediction. How much of that excess is algorithmically recoverable is
open; \textsc{rd-encoder ridge} recovers a measurable fraction without
retraining ($0.22 \to 0.094$ on Llama, $3$-seed mean). The headline for practitioners
is the five-rule decision tree of \S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}.
