Merging several LoRA adapters into one model sometimes costs almost
nothing and sometimes destroys a task outright, and practitioners have
no cheap way to tell which case they are in before they merge. We give
a closed-form lower bound on worst-task negative log-likelihood excess
for rank-$r$ merging, together with a matching achievability
construction. The bound splits into an irreducible floor
$B^2\bigl(1 - \deff/(Tr)\bigr)$, governed by the effective dimension
$\deff$ of the union of the task subspaces, and a rate term.

Our first finding is a negative one about our own bound. On real LoRA
cohorts the floor is not merely small: it is \emph{exactly zero}, on
every base model and under every initialisation scheme we tried, because
$T$ rank-$r$ adapters with $Tr \leq d$ have linearly independent row
spaces even when those spaces are nearly collinear. The floor term is
therefore vacuous for LoRA merging as practised, and the interesting
content of the bound lies elsewhere.

Where it lies is conditioning. Whether a cohort's adapters share one
initialisation of the LoRA $A$ factor, a detail almost never reported,
moves the median principal cosine between task subspaces from $0.047$ to
$0.995$ and the condition number of the merge operator by four to five
orders of magnitude, from $1.5$ to between $1.8\times10^{4}$ and
$8.3\times10^{4}$. The exact interpolator is then $43$ to $64$ times
larger than the mean task delta, against $T$ under independent
initialisation, the orthogonal-subspace value, which the measurement
returns to within $1.4\%$ at both $T=3$ and $T=4$. The whole diagnosis costs about one CPU minute and needs no
forward passes.

We then test, under pre-registration, whether this has operational
consequences, and report the outcome whichever way it fell. It is
invisible to standard merging heuristics: across twenty base-by-method
cells, task arithmetic, TIES, DARE and TVQ are indifferent to which
regime the cohort occupies, because all of them produce bounded-norm,
near-mean solutions that never approach the ill-conditioned direction.
It is severe for methods that solve the linear system: unregularised,
the same merge is $4.3$ to $65$ times worse on the ill-conditioned
cohort, and a single ridge term repairs it. Along the way our own
encoder's advantage did not survive the move to properly initialised
cohorts, a pre-registered control we wrote ourselves withdrew a headline
claim about benchmark confounding, and a rate-exponent falsification we
had reported turned out to rest on a mismatched reference and is
withdrawn. What survives is the bound, the diagnosis, and the audit.
