Merging several LoRA adapters into one model sometimes costs almost
nothing and sometimes destroys a task, and practitioners have no cheap way
to tell which case they are in beforehand. We prove a closed-form lower
bound on worst-task negative log-likelihood excess for rank-$r$ merging,
with a matching achievability construction: an irreducible floor
$B^2(1 - \deff/(Tr))$, governed by the effective dimension $\deff$ of the
union of the task subspaces, plus a rate term decaying as
$2^{-2R/\deff}$. On real cohorts that floor turns out to be vacuous,
exactly zero under every initialisation scheme we tried, because $T$
rank-$r$ adapters with $Tr \leq d$ span independently even when nearly
collinear. What the bound does expose is conditioning. Whether a cohort's
adapters share one initialisation of the LoRA $A$ factor, a detail almost
never reported, moves the median principal cosine between task subspaces
from $0.047$ to $0.995$ and the condition number of the merge operator
from $1.5$ to as much as $8.3\times10^{4}$. Diagnosing it costs about one
CPU minute and no forward passes. We pre-registered whether this matters
and report both halves. It is invisible to standard heuristics, which
produce bounded-norm near-mean solutions: across twenty base-by-method
cells we detect no effect at our $0.01$ nat resolution. It is decisive for
methods that solve the linear system, which reach $35$ to $57$ times the
mean task delta with three quarters of their energy in the ill-conditioned
directions: unregularised they are $2.1$ to $65$ times worse, $25$ to
$117$ without rank truncation, and one ridge term repairs it, on two
solvers of which one is not ours. Several of our own claims did not
survive, including our encoder's advantage and this metric's link to
downstream accuracy, and we report those at full strength.
