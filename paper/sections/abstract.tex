Merging LoRA adapters into a single deployable model is judged
empirically: methods are compared on benchmarks with no notion of how
much room is left to improve, and methods that work on one base
sometimes invert to worst-of-the-matrix on another with no warning. We
attack both problems with a single tool. A worst-task rate-distortion
analysis of LoRA merging produces a closed-form lower bound on
per-coordinate NLL excess, $\Phi^\star \geq B^2 (1 - d_{\mathrm{eff}}
/ (Tr))$ in the data-free quadratic-loss limit, that admits an
exact-construction encoder; on real adapters the exact construction
fails for a measured spectral reason, but a one-line Tikhonov ridge
salvages it into \textsc{rd-encoder ridge} --- a merge method that ties
or beats every published baseline at $T = 4$ on the
$4$-base $\times$ $7$-method matrix we evaluate, and widens its lead
at $T = 7$ (excess ratio vs.\ Task-Arithmetic $0.84 \to 0.68 \to 0.52$
on Llama-3.1-Instruct). The same analysis yields a $1$-CPU-minute audit
of any trained adapter cohort that diagnoses when classical methods
such as TIES invert from best to worst: a perturbation derivation of
the TIES sign-election update predicts an ambiguity window
$R^\star \in [0.025, 0.075]$ for the per-task win-share range, which
the two bases that bracket it (Yi-Chat $R = 0.077$ inverts;
Llama-Instruct $R = 0.028$ does not) confirm and a pre-registered third
base (Mistral-7B at $R = 0.041$) is set to test. Across
$4 \text{ bases} \times 2 \text{ downstream metrics}$, NLL ranking
predicts accuracy ranking on $7$ of $8$ cells at Spearman
$\rho \geq +0.72$. The headline for practitioners is the five-rule
decision tree of \S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}: in the
floor-zero regime that every cohort we measured occupies, the entire
$0.10$--$0.22$ nats of worst-task excess is recoverable algorithmic
slack, and the encoder we construct claims a measurable fraction of it
without retraining.
