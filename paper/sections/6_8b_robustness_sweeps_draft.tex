\subsection{Robustness: tuning the closest competitors}
\label{subsec:exp-robustness}

The unified head-to-head of Table~\ref{tab:e10-baselines} compares every
method at its \emph{default} hyperparameter (TIES/DARE density $=0.2$,
RegMean ridge $\lambda = 10^{-3}$). A natural objection is that the default
handicaps the baselines: perhaps a tuned TIES or a tuned RegMean would erase
rd-encoder ridge's lead. We test this directly by sweeping the single key
hyperparameter of each of the three closest competitors and comparing rd-encoder
ridge against each method's \emph{best} setting per base. Throughout this
subsection we hold rd-encoder ridge at its $3$-seed mean
($0.094/0.044/0.010/0.037$ on Llama/Mistral/Qwen/Yi) --- the conservative
figure, since its seed-$1$ cell is lower ($0.082/0.047/0.010/0.036$) --- so the
comparison is, if anything, biased \emph{against} our method.

\paragraph{Density sweep (TIES, DARE).}
We sweep merge density over $\{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$ for both TIES and
DARE on all four bases (seed-$1$, identical eval pipeline to
Table~\ref{tab:e10-baselines}; $32$ cells). Table~\ref{tab:density-sweep}
reports each method's best density per base. rd-encoder ridge retains the
lowest worst-task excess on Llama, Mistral, and Qwen even against each method's
\emph{tuned} density; on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat, TIES at its best density ($0.1$)
reaches $0.036$, edging rd-encoder ridge's $3$-seed mean ($0.037$) by a margin
($0.001$) far inside the $\pm 0.004$ seed-noise floor --- a statistical tie.
The default $0.2$ is near-optimal for TIES on three of four bases, so the main
matrix did not materially sandbag it.

\paragraph{RegMean ridge-$\lambda$ sweep.}
RegMean is rd-encoder ridge's closest structural relative (both are data-free,
ridge-regularized centroids with different projectors), so the default
$\lambda = 10^{-3}$ comparison conflates the centroid with the regularizer
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-e10}). We sweep RegMean's $\lambda$ over six decades
$\{10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}, 1, 10\}$ on all four bases (seed-$1$,
$20$ cells); the response is U-shaped with a minimum at $\lambda \in
[10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]$. Table~\ref{tab:regmean-lambda} reports RegMean's best
$\lambda$ per base. With RegMean tuned, rd-encoder ridge still wins on Llama,
Mistral, and Qwen, but \emph{loses} on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat.

\paragraph{Three matched seeds settle the close calls.}
The Qwen and Yi margins above are small, so we re-ran rd-encoder ridge and
best-$\lambda$ RegMean at \emph{three} matched training seeds on those two
bases (on identical adapters, removing the seed-$1$-vs-\texttt{v1} adapter-set
mismatch that the published rd-ridge cells had carried). Both verdicts are
confident --- all three seeds agree in sign:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-7B:} rd-encoder ridge $0.0097$ vs RegMean $0.0112$ ---
  rd-encoder ridge wins on all three seeds.
\item \textbf{Yi-1.5-9B-Chat:} rd-encoder ridge $0.0366$ vs RegMean $0.0328$ ---
  a tuned RegMean wins on all three seeds (per-seed gap $-0.003$ to $-0.005$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What we conclude.}
rd-encoder ridge's advantage is \emph{robust to competitor hyperparameter
tuning on three of four bases} (Llama, Mistral, Qwen): no tuned density and no
tuned $\lambda$ lets TIES, DARE, or RegMean overtake it there. On
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat --- the one base we independently flag as saturated, where
NLL-excess comparisons amplify per-task asymmetries that need not track
deployable quality (\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}, R2) --- rd-encoder ridge is
merely competitive: a tuned RegMean beats it and a tuned TIES ties it. We
therefore claim rd-encoder ridge as the strongest data-free merge \emph{robustly
on the non-saturated bases}, and as competitive-but-not-dominant on the
saturated one. This is a narrower claim than ``best on all four,'' and it is the
honest one.
