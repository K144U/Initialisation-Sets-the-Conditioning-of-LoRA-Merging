Fusing several low-rank adapters (LoRA) trained on one base model into a
single model sometimes costs almost nothing and sometimes destroys a task,
with no way to tell in advance. We identify a property of the adapter set
that decides this for an important class of fusion methods. When the adapters
share one random initialisation of the LoRA $A$ factor, which a single
training seed arranges and which the default settings of Unsloth and
LLaMA-Factory impose, their task subspaces collapse onto nearly the same
directions: the median principal cosine is 0.99, against 0.05 for independent
draws. A first-order theory, confirmed on real adapters to three significant
figures, shows why this matters: the condition number of the fused problem
grows as the inverse square of how far the adapters drift apart in training,
and the exact interpolator as its inverse. Pre-registered tests on four base
models and eleven fusion methods, nine of which we did not design, locate the
damage: merges that solve a linear system built from the adapters' own
factors fail, down to zero accuracy on GSM8K and HumanEval, while averaging,
sparsifying, whitening, and solvers built from data are unaffected. One ridge
term restores the affected merges. The collapsed regime can be read from the
adapter files in about a CPU minute, and it occurs in 37.8\% of 45 public
LoRA cohorts, including the language cohort released with a published merging
method. Several of our predictions failed, among them that the failure would
be specific to the collapsed regime on public adapters, and we report those
alongside the ones that held.
