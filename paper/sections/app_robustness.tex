\subsection{The $\lambda$-sweep and held-out $\lambda$ check}
\label{app:lambda-sweep}

Table~\ref{tab:rd-ridge-sweep} reports the full Tikhonov sweep on
Llama-3.1-8B behind Figure~\ref{fig:salvage}. The curve is a clean U
with a broad minimum: $\lambda \in [0.05, 0.07]$ all fall within
$0.005$ of the optimum, at the seed-noise scale. At the optimum the
per-task excess is balanced ($+0.094$ GSM8K, $+0.024$ Alpaca, $+0.032$
Magicoder, $+0.018$ Translation), versus the near-unregularized form
($\lambda = 0.001$) where Translation alone took $+2.88$ nats from
sliver amplification.

A fair objection to any swept hyperparameter is that $\lambda^\star$
is selected on the same worst-task NLL the authors report the win on. Two
answers. First, the win is robust across the entire band, not a tuned
needle: rd-encoder ridge sits below the $3$-seed TIES mean for every
$\lambda \in [0.03, 0.18]$ on Llama, and the sweep is
monotone-then-flat on the other three bases. Second, a single
globally-fixed $\lambda = 0.13$, the leave-one-base-out choice
(modal optimum of the other three bases) with no per-base selection,
already beats TA and TIES on all four bases
(Table~\ref{tab:rd-ridge-heldout}); per-base tuning is a refinement
that helps only Llama.

\paragraph{Fisher-diagonal $H_t$: sanity check on the surrogate.}
Replacing the projector surrogate with the diagonal of the empirical
Fisher (input-activation second moment on $512$ training examples per
task), the centroid reduces in closed form to a per-column
Fisher-weighted task arithmetic. Worst-task excess: $0.210$ at
$\lambda = 0$ (barely beating TA) and $0.319$ at $\lambda = 0.1$:
the regularization effect \emph{reverses}. The authors read this as Fisher's
per-coordinate weight already implementing a form of sliver
suppression, so an additive identity floor degrades that structure
rather than supplying a missing one; the projector surrogate paired
with ridge is empirically preferred.

\subsection{Tuning the closest competitors}
\label{subsec:exp-robustness}

Table~\ref{tab:e10-baselines} compares every method at its default
hyperparameter (TIES/DARE density $0.2$, RegMean ridge
$\lambda = 10^{-3}$). The authors sweep the single key hyperparameter of each
of the three closest competitors and compare rd-encoder ridge against
each method's \emph{best} setting per base, holding rd-encoder ridge
at its $3$-seed mean (the conservative figure; its seed-$1$ cells are
lower).

\paragraph{Density sweep (TIES, DARE).} Sweeping density over
$\{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5\}$ on all four bases (seed-$1$, $32$
cells; Table~\ref{tab:density-sweep}): rd-encoder ridge retains the
lowest worst-task excess on Llama, Mistral, and Qwen against each
method's tuned density; on Yi, TIES at its best density ($0.1$)
reaches $0.036$, edging rd-encoder ridge's $0.037$ by a margin far
inside the $\pm 0.004$ seed-noise floor, a statistical tie. The
default $0.2$ is near-optimal for TIES on three of four bases, so the
main matrix did not materially sandbag it.

\paragraph{RegMean ridge-$\lambda$ sweep.} RegMean is rd-encoder
ridge's closest structural relative (both are data-free,
ridge-regularised centroids with different projectors), so the authors sweep
its $\lambda$ over six decades $\{10^{-4}, \dots, 10\}$ on all four
bases; the response is U-shaped with a minimum at
$\lambda \in [10^{-3}, 10^{-2}]$. With RegMean tuned
(Table~\ref{tab:regmean-lambda}), rd-encoder ridge still wins on
Llama, Mistral, and Qwen, but \emph{loses} on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat.

\paragraph{Three matched seeds settle the close calls.} The Qwen and
Yi margins are small, so the authors re-ran rd-encoder ridge and best-$\lambda$
RegMean at three matched training seeds on identical adapters. Both
verdicts are confident, with all three seeds agreeing in sign: on
Qwen-2.5-7B rd-encoder ridge wins ($0.0097$ vs.\ $0.0112$) on every
seed; on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat tuned RegMean wins ($0.0328$ vs.\ $0.0366$;
per-seed gap $-0.003$ to $-0.005$) on every seed.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} rd-encoder ridge's advantage is robust to
competitor hyperparameter tuning on three of four bases; on saturated
Yi-Chat, the one base where the authors independently flag NLL excess as an
unreliable proxy (R2), it is merely competitive: a tuned RegMean
beats it and a tuned TIES ties it. The authors claim the strongest data-free
merge \emph{robustly on the non-saturated bases}, and
competitive-but-not-dominant on the saturated one.
