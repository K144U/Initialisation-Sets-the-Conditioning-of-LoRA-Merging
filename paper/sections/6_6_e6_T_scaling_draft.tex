\subsection{Does the algorithmic ordering hold beyond $T=4$?}
\label{subsec:exp-T-scaling}

The matrix of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}--\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}
fixes $T=4$. To test whether the algorithmic ordering and the ridge
salvage survive as $T$ grows on real LoRA adapters, we run a $T$-sweep
on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat across $T \in \{2, 4, 7\}$ — the real-data
complement to E4's synthetic prediction of $\log T$ scaling with
slope $\rho \approx 1 / \sqrt{r}$.

\paragraph{Setup.} We trained four new rank-$16$ LoRA adapters
(CodeAlpaca, Dolly, XSum, plus retained Math-style on the GSM8K
prompt format) and merged them with the four \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}
adapters (Alpaca, Magicoder, GSM8K, FLORES translation), all on
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat. Six merge methods cover the matrix: Task Arithmetic
(TA), TIES, DARE, KnOTS, TVQ at $b=2$, and rd-encoder with
ridge $\lambda = 0.13$ (Yi-optimal from
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}). For each $T$ we draw one fixed
nested chain plus three random subsets at $T \in \{2, 4\}$, and use
the all-$7$-tasks subset at $T = 7$. Each cell evaluates worst-task
NLL excess at $n_{\text{eval}} = 1000$. Total: $54$ merge cells.

\paragraph{Headline: ordering persists, but with two surprises.}
Table~\ref{tab:e6-worst} reports worst-task NLL excess, averaged
across subsets at each $T$.

\paragraph{Surprise 1: TIES inverts at $T = 7$.}
The dip champion of \S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed} (TIES, lowest excess
on Yi at $T = 4$ across most subsets) becomes the \emph{worst} method
at $T = 7$ ($0.148$ vs.\ $0.111$--$0.130$ for the others). The
inversion holds on the single $T = 7$ subset we have and is
consistent with the slope reading (Table~\ref{tab:e6-slopes}): TIES
has the steepest log-$T$ slope ($0.098$, $R^2 = 0.9995$). The
mechanism we suspect is sign-election contamination: at small $T$,
TIES's per-coordinate sign vote is dominated by a few task-specific
adapters; as $T$ grows the vote averages toward consensus signs that
no single task wants. We do not test this further here; it joins the
candidate-mechanism list for the L3 anomaly of
\S\ref{subsec:exp-downstream-gsm8k}.

\paragraph{Surprise 2: log-$T$ slope is $3$--$7\times$ shallower than
E4 synthetic predicts.}
Table~\ref{tab:e6-slopes} fits $\text{worst-excess}(T) = a + b \log T$
per method. E4's synthetic estimate from
\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-synthetic} gave $b \approx 1/\sqrt{r} = 0.25$ at
$r = 16$. The measured slopes on all-cells data are
$b \in [0.070, 0.098]$ — uniformly smaller. On alpaca-OUT cells (the
unconfounded subset) they are smaller still, $b \in [0.031, 0.053]$,
though we only have two $T$ values there. The functional form
holds ($R^2 \geq 0.96$ for $5$ of $6$ methods on all-cells) but the
constant is friendlier than the synthetic estimate. We conjecture
that real LoRA adapters have substantial column-space overlap
(unlike i.i.d.\ Stiefel-random columns assumed in E4's model), which
reduces inter-task interference and slows the $\log T$ growth.

\paragraph{TVQ $b{=}2$ dip survives the $T$-sweep.}
TVQ $b{=}2$ — the dip champion of \S\ref{subsec:exp-b2-mechanism} —
is essentially flat in $T$ (slope $-0.001$ on all-cells, $-0.088$ on
alpaca-OUT) and at $T = 7$ becomes the best method ($0.111$, tied
with rd-ridge). This is consistent with the implicit-TIES
interpretation of \S\ref{subsec:exp-b2-mechanism}: the
quantization-induced sign election is a $T$-robust mechanism, and
the dip is not a $T = 4$ artifact.

\paragraph{Why we split on Alpaca.}
The all-cells $T = 4$ slot showed rd-ridge \emph{regressing} relative
to TA ($0.084$ vs.\ $0.067$), apparently contradicting
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}'s salvage result. The mechanism is
specific to the Alpaca adapter: on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat (already
instruction-tuned), the Alpaca v1 LoRA improves per-task NLL by only
$\tau - \text{base} = 0.857 - 0.971 = 0.114$ — less than half the
headroom of any other E6 adapter (CodeAlpaca $0.236$, GSM8K $0.554$,
Dolly $0.531$). Excess NLL is reported relative to $\tau$, so a
small absolute perturbation is a large relative excess specifically
for Alpaca. The ridge encoder's projection step costs Alpaca
disproportionately: averaged over the three alpaca-IN $T = 4$
subsets, $\text{excess}_{\text{Alpaca}} = 0.097$ for rd-ridge vs.\
$0.032$ for TA --- a $3 \times$ asymmetry. The per-layer
$\Delta$-cosine analysis on the $\binom{5}{2}$ adapter pairs confirms
Alpaca is the most isolated of the cohort (mean cosine $0.028$ to the
other four, vs.\ Dolly $0.097$); combined with the small headroom,
even a faithful projection leaves Alpaca with little signal to
salvage. We therefore report both views and use alpaca-OUT as the
fairer estimate of the encoder's behavior on adapters trained under
homogeneous conditions.

\paragraph{Alpaca-OUT: the ridge salvage persists at $T = 4$.}
On the four alpaca-OUT subsets ($3$ at $T = 2$, $1$ at $T = 4$),
rd-ridge attains the lowest worst-task excess at $T = 4$ ($0.045$ vs.\
TA $0.056$, TIES $0.058$, KnOTS $0.056$). This matches the
\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder} cross-model finding that rd-ridge
salvages TA at $T = 4$ on Yi at $\lambda = 0.13$; the $\S 6.2$
result is not contradicted by E6. We caution that the alpaca-OUT
$T = 4$ slot has only one subset and the alpaca-OUT cohort has no
$T = 7$ cell at all (every $T = 7$ subset by construction includes
Alpaca). We therefore do not extend the slope claim past $T = 4$ on
alpaca-OUT data.

\paragraph{What this changes for the paper.}
Three things.
(i) The $T$-scaling story closes the theory$\to$synthetic$\to$real
loop, but with a measured friendlier constant: the empirical
log-$T$ slope on real Yi-1.5-9B adapters is $0.03$--$0.10$, well
below the synthetic $0.25$. We report this as a quantitative
refinement of E4's $\rho \cdot \log T$ scaling rather than a
contradiction of it.
(ii) The TIES inversion at $T = 7$ is a new finding worth a
falsifiable conjecture: sign-election consensus degrades faster
than coordinate magnitude under increasing $T$. We propose this for
follow-up in the future-work section.
(iii) The Alpaca shallow-adapter artifact is a methodological
warning: excess NLL is informative for adapter cohorts trained under
matched conditions but is sensitive to per-task headroom asymmetry.
Practitioners selecting a merge method on a chat-tuned base should
either renormalize excess by per-task headroom or remove
near-saturated adapters from the cohort. We will add this to
\S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}.
