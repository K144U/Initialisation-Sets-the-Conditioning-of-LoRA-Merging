\subsection{Does the algorithmic ordering hold beyond $T=4$?}
\label{subsec:exp-T-scaling}

The matrix of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora}--\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}
fixes $T=4$. To test whether the algorithmic ordering and the
ridge-encoder salvage survive as $T$ grows on real LoRA adapters, we
run a $T$-sweep on \emph{two} base models across $T \in \{2, 4, 7\}$:
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat (E1's easiest base, $\lambda = 0.13$) and
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (E1's hardest base, $\lambda = 0.05$). The
sweeps are the real-data complement to E4's synthetic prediction of
log-$T$ growth with slope $\rho \approx 1 / \sqrt{r}$.

\paragraph{Setup.} For each base, we trained three new rank-$16$ LoRA
adapters (CodeAlpaca, Dolly, XSum) and merged them with the four
\S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} adapters (Alpaca, Magicoder, GSM8K, FLORES
translation). Six merge methods cover the matrix: Task Arithmetic
(TA), TIES, DARE, KnOTS, TVQ at $b=2$, and rd-encoder ridge at the
base-optimal $\lambda$ of \S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}. For each $T$
we draw one fixed nested chain plus three random subsets at
$T \in \{2, 4\}$ and use the all-$7$-tasks subset at $T = 7$. Each
cell evaluates worst-task NLL excess at $n_{\text{eval}} = 1000$.
Total: $108$ merge cells ($54$ per base, identical subset
composition for cross-base comparison).

\paragraph{Headline: ordering holds, rd-ridge salvage grows with $T$.}
Table~\ref{tab:e6-worst} reports worst-task NLL excess, averaged
across subsets at each $T$ per base.

On Llama, rd-encoder ridge is the best method at every $T$, and the
margin widens as $T$ grows: ratio rd-ridge/TA $= 0.84$ at $T = 2$,
$0.68$ at $T = 4$, $0.52$ at $T = 7$. \S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}'s
cross-model salvage result is not just preserved at $T = 7$, it
strengthens. On Yi, rd-ridge wins on alpaca-OUT subsets and at
$T = 7$ all-cells; the all-cells regression at $T = 4$ is the
Alpaca-artifact paragraph below.

\paragraph{Log-$T$ form holds on both bases, with base-dependent
slope.}
Table~\ref{tab:e6-slopes} fits $\text{worst-excess}(T) = a + b \log T$
per (base, method). E4's synthetic estimate from
\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-synthetic} gave $b \approx 1 / \sqrt{r} = 0.25$
at $r = 16$. All measured slopes are positive (except Yi TVQ$_2$,
which we discuss below), the functional form holds
($R^2 \geq 0.93$ on $11$ of $12$ all-cells fits), but the constant
is base-dependent: Llama slopes are $\sim 2 \times$ larger than Yi's
and within $1.6 \times$ of synthetic. The five matrix-merging
methods (TA, TIES, DARE, KnOTS, TVQ$_2$) cluster tightly within each
base. Rd-ridge has the lowest slope on both bases ($0.070$ on Yi,
$0.065$ on Llama), confirming that the ridge regularization buys
$T$-stability across very different base models.

\paragraph{Three Yi-specific artifacts the Llama sweep rules out.}
The Yi-only v1 of this analysis flagged three findings that the
Llama sweep now identifies as base-dependent rather than universal:

\textbf{(A) TIES inversion at $T = 7$ is Yi-specific, driven by
per-task representation imbalance.} On Yi, TIES becomes the worst
method at $T = 7$ ($0.148$, slope $0.098$, $R^2 = 0.9995$). On
Llama, TIES at $T = 7$ is $0.232$, third-best of six, in the
natural ordering. We tested the v1 conjecture (``sign-election
consensus degrades faster on Yi than Llama'') with a direct
per-coordinate sign-vote audit on both $T = 7$ cohorts: trim each
adapter's $\Delta$ at density $0.2$ (the TIES default), stack the
$T = 7$ trimmed deltas, and tally per-coordinate (\#positive,
\#negative) votes plus per-task win-share (fraction of coords where
that task's sign matches the elected sign).
Table~\ref{tab:ties-probe} reports the result.

The refined mechanism: TIES sign-election on a saturated base
produces a \emph{directionally biased} consensus, not a degraded
one. On Yi-Chat, six adapters whose tasks share substantial
instruction-following structure (Alpaca, CodeAlpaca, Dolly, GSM8K,
Translation, XSum) vote in a common subspace; Magicoder (code,
$0.776$ win share) and Alpaca (near-saturated, $0.785$) are
systematically outvoted. The resulting merge biases toward the
instruction-following consensus at the cost of code-specific and
saturated-direction signal. On Llama-3.1-Instruct, the per-task
votes are nearly uniform ($0.805$--$0.832$), so TIES preserves
each task's contribution to within a tighter band. We update the
v1 conjecture to: \emph{TIES underperforms when the per-task
sign-election win-share range exceeds a threshold (empirically
$\sim 0.05$); this happens on heavily-aligned chat-tuned bases
where adapter $\Delta$ tensors share a dominant
instruction-following direction}. Falsifying experiment for future
work: synthetic adapters with controlled subspace overlap should
reproduce the inversion when the overlap crosses the
win-share-range threshold, independent of the base.

\textbf{(B) TVQ-$b{=}2$'s flat-in-$T$ behavior is Yi-specific.} On
Yi all-cells, TVQ$_2$ slope is $-0.001$ (essentially flat); on
alpaca-OUT cells it is $-0.088$ (decreasing). On Llama, TVQ$_2$
slope is $+0.119$, comparable to the other matrix methods. The
\S\ref{subsec:exp-b2-mechanism} dip is therefore not a $T$-robust
mechanism in general; it is a Yi$_2$-specific stability where the
$b{=}2$ sign-election noise happens to track the shallow-adapter
direction. Llama's $b{=}2$ degrades with $T$ like any other
quantization-loss method, just slower.

\textbf{(C) Alpaca shallow-adapter artifact is Yi-specific.} On Yi,
the $T = 4$ all-cells rd-ridge regression ($0.084$ vs.\ TA $0.067$)
is driven entirely by Alpaca: Yi-Chat's per-task headroom from base
to $\tau$ is just $0.114$ on Alpaca, compared to $0.236$--$0.554$
for the other E6 adapters; any merge perturbation is large
\emph{relative} to that small headroom. Llama-3.1-Instruct's Alpaca
headroom is $0.246$, double Yi's, and the all-cells T = 4 rd-ridge
result on Llama ($0.104$) is the best of any method --- no
artifact. Per-layer $\Delta$-cosine analysis on Yi confirmed Alpaca
was the most isolated of the cohort (mean cosine $0.028$); on Llama
the artifact does not appear because the headroom is sufficient to
absorb the projection cost.

\paragraph{Why the cross-model picture is cleaner than Yi alone.}
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat is heavily aligned: low Alpaca headroom, near-zero
TIES inversion at $T = 7$, flat TVQ$_2$ in $T$. Each of these is a
saturation symptom: when a base is already nearly optimal on an
instruction-following task, any small perturbation looks large
relative to the residual headroom, and method-specific failure
modes (sign-election contamination, quantization-induced sparsity
noise) get amplified. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is also instruction-
tuned but with more residual NLL headroom across the cohort, so
the cross-base sweep recovers the clean log-$T$ scaling that
synthetic data already showed. The cross-model evidence promotes
rd-encoder ridge from "ties at best" (Yi all-cells $T = 7$) to
"clear winner with widening margin" (Llama $T \in \{2, 4, 7\}$).

\paragraph{What this changes for the paper.}
Four updates to v1.
(i) The $T$-scaling form holds on both bases ($R^2 \geq 0.93$ for
$11$ of $12$ all-cells fits); the slope is base-dependent: Yi
$\in [0.07, 0.10]$, Llama $\in [0.12, 0.16]$. We report this as a
quantitative refinement of E4's $\rho \cdot \log T$, with the
empirical $\rho$ between half and two-thirds of the synthetic
$1 / \sqrt{r}$ estimate.
(ii) Rd-encoder ridge is the only method whose log-$T$ slope is
nearly base-invariant ($0.070$ on Yi, $0.065$ on Llama), and on
Llama it dominates TA by an increasing margin as $T$ grows
($16\% \to 32\% \to 48\%$ improvements). This promotes the ridge
result from "salvage at $T = 4$" to "the $T$-stable method on
real adapters."
(iii) The three Yi-only surprises (TIES inversion at $T = 7$,
TVQ$_2$ flat in $T$, Alpaca shallow-adapter artifact) are unified
under one explanation: \emph{Yi-Chat is near-saturated on
instruction-following tasks}, which compresses the per-task
headroom and amplifies method-specific noise. The TIES inversion
mechanism is now a measured result, not a conjecture: the per-task
sign-election win-share range is $2.8 \times$ wider on Yi than on
Llama (Table~\ref{tab:ties-probe}), so TIES's elected consensus is
directionally biased rather than degraded. We move the three
findings from ``Yi anomaly'' to a \emph{base-saturation regime}
caveat for the practical recommendations of
\S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}.
(iv) The Alpaca artifact remains a methodological warning for
practitioners: a chat-tuned base can hide method-specific
degradations behind low headroom on the saturated task. Renormalize
excess by per-task headroom, or exclude near-saturated adapters,
when comparing methods on a heavily instruction-tuned base.
