**W1. A tuned merge coefficient matches the method.** Seed1, matched adapters.

| base | TA @ 0.25 (paper default) | best TA | α\* | rd-ridge 3-seed | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Llama-3.1 | 0.2139 | **0.0839** | 0.75 | 0.0945 | **−0.0106 tuned TA wins** |
| Mistral-7B | 0.1328 | 0.0631 | 0.50 | 0.0441 | +0.0190 rd-ridge |

| Yi-1.5 | 0.0990 | 0.0530 | 0.50 | 0.0366 | +0.0164 rd-ridge |

Upheld on 1 of 4, below the pre-registered ">=2 restates the contribution"
threshold, so the method survives. But:
- margins go from **56-91% below TA** to **−13% to 39% below a tuned TA**;
- §6.2 finding (3) "only TIES separates from the structure-blind cluster"
  **fails on Llama**: tuned TA 0.0839 beats TIES 0.1539;
- even under a symmetric global-constant rule (TA fixed at 0.50 vs rd-ridge at
  its fixed λ=0.13) rd-ridge **still loses Llama**, 0.1250 vs 0.1184;
- TA's optimum is 0.50-0.75, so **1/T = 0.25 is undertuned on all four bases**.

The 8 pending cells make this 3-seed vs 3-seed. Llama's rd-ridge seed spread
(0.0823 to 0.0945) is *larger* than the gap being argued about, so the Llama
verdict genuinely hangs on it.

**W1/V2. The norm is load-bearing.** Renormalising rd-ridge to TA's Frobenius
norm costs **+0.142 nats** (0.0822 → 0.2240), worse than TA itself. Yet 84% of
W\*'s mass is orthogonal to any rescaling of TA. Both true at once: it is not a
scaled TA, but its advantage needs its 2.44x norm.

EM **0.326 → 0.788**, extraction failures **300/500 → 0/500**. The published

have been caught then. Expect all 8 downstream cells and all 8 Spearman values

### 3.2 Confirmed by measurement (already done, no GPU)

**A1. All T adapters per cohort share one LoRA `A` init.** `train_lora.py`
seeds from `seeds.global`, identical across the four task configs; LoRA starts
`B = 0` so `A` barely moves. All four bases:

| | across tasks (same seed) | across seeds (same task) |
|---|---|---|
| `‖ΔA‖/‖A‖` | 0.16-0.20 | 1.41 (= √2, independent) |
| principal cosines | median **0.996** | 0.054 |

Participation ratio **16.3 of Tr = 64**, σ_max = 1.999 ≈ √T. The subspaces are
near-**coincident**: the max-overlap floor-positive regime Appendix B argues
cannot arise. `hard d_eff = Tr` holds only because the rank tolerance is
~1e-3·σ₁. The T=7 pool is worse: geometrically two 16-dim subspaces (4 v1
adapters + 3 pilot adapters from a different training run).

**W2 is answered and it is good news:** the hard floor is flat across four
decades of threshold and stays under 0.015 B² even at ε = 0.1
(`measure_subspace_geometry.py --cohort seed1`). But the threshold-free soft
floor is 0.745 B², so the reviewer is right for a reason he did not identify:
rank is the wrong stability class for this geometry.

**A3. rd-ridge is deployed at rank 64, baselines at rank 16.** Headline cells
use `realize="rank_deff"` (= Tr = 64) while the λ=0 comparison cell uses rank
16, so the salvage arc is confounded. Rank-16 rd-ridge exists and still wins
(Llama 0.0849), so this is cheap to fix; W1 group C re-runs it properly.

**A4. The HumanEval harness discarded up to 79% of generations.**
`_strip_humaneval_completion` returned "" for markdown-fenced answers: 122-130
of 164 empty for TA/DARE/KnOTS vs 0-11 for TIES/TVQ2/rd-ridge. Fixed;
conditional pass@1 narrows the claimed "2-40x" gap to about 2-9x.

**B1. Train/eval overlap ~14.5% (alpaca), ~10% (magicoder).** Training shuffles
with `seeds.global` (1/2/3), eval cells with `seed: 20260518`, and
`load_task_split` only guarantees disjointness when both use the same seed. The
Reproducibility Statement's zero-overlap claim is **false as written**.
`train_lora.py` now has `seeds.data` (defaults to `seeds.global`, so nothing
existing changed); the A1 cohort pins it to the eval seed.

**W3. The rate axis is untested with the ridge on.** Every rd-ridge cell is
`bits=32`. The only real-data rate curve, `eval_e1/` at λ=0, is non-monotone on
4 of 4 bases and 9-11 nats on Mistral, and has never appeared in the paper. W3
runs b ∈ {1,2,3,4,8,16} with the ridge on. Smoke gave q(4) = +0.0023 nats, so
**b=8 may fall below noise** and the fit may rest on b ∈ {2,3,4}.

**W4. Every T=7 point is one merge cell, one seed.** The Yi TIES inversion gap
is 0.0197 against a T=4 subset-to-subset range of 0.017-0.019 on the same base;
it may not replicate. Log-T fits are 2 parameters on 3 points.

**W6. Three different numbers for one quantity.** Llama TA is 0.218 (§6.2, v1
cohort), 0.213 (Table 1, seed-1), 0.220 (Fig 2/App G, 3-seed). Table 1's five
matrix baselines are seed-1 but the caption says so only for the four *added*
ones, while rd-ridge in the same table is a 3-seed mean. Also: with n=5,
Spearman can only take 1 − Σd²/20, so the paper's Mistral GSM8K **+0.67 is not
attainable**; the correct value is +0.60.

## 7. THE HONEST BOTTOM LINE

The theory is untouched and still stands. What broke is the empirical case: the
headline margin was largely an undertuned baseline, one baseline was a no-op,
both accuracy metrics were mis-scored, and the geometric claim measures a shared
initialisation rather than a property of fine-tuning. Every one of those is
fixable and most fixes are already running. But the paper that exists today
makes claims the data does not support, and the next version has to be visibly
more modest: **rd-encoder ridge beats a coefficient-tuned Task Arithmetic on
three of four bases by 30-39%, and ties or loses on the fourth.** That is still
worth publishing. It is not the result currently written.
