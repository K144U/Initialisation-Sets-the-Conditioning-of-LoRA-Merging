\subsection{The added closed-form baselines}
\label{subsec:exp-e10}\label{subsec:exp-e10-e11}

The unified head-to-head of Table~\ref{tab:e10-baselines} (main text)
adds four methods commonly requested as merge-paper baselines:
\textbf{Fisher-magnitude} (per-coordinate weighted average with
$F_t[i] = \Delta_t[i]^2$ as the Fisher proxy, the cheap non-gradient
variant of~\citet{matena2022fisher}), \textbf{DELLA}~\citep{della2024}
(DARE-style random drop with rescale, then TIES density trim and sign
election), and the two data-free closed-form relatives of rd-encoder
ridge: \textbf{RegMean}~\citep{jin2023regmean} (data-free variant using
$A_t^\top A_t$ as the Gram surrogate) and
\textbf{AdaMerging}~\citep{adamerging2024} (data-free variant with
per-task $\alpha \propto \|\Delta_t\|_F$). Each runs a seed-$1$ cell on
each base at $T = 4$ with the same merge cohort. RegMean is competitive
on the easier bases but collapses on Llama ($0.243$, worse than TA)
through the same sliver-eigenvalue pathology that breaks our own
unregularized encoder ($0.340$, \S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder});
AdaMerging trails throughout; Fisher and DELLA land in the second tier,
never inverting the R1/R3 ordering. All four are gradient-free
closed-form merges at TA-level cost, so none is a compute barrier.
Because the default-hyperparameter comparison conflates the
rate-distortion centroid with the ridge regularization, the clean
control (sweeping RegMean's own ridge $\lambda$ on matched seeds)
is run in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}; with RegMean tuned, rd-encoder
ridge still wins on Llama, Mistral, and Qwen but \emph{loses} on
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat ($0.037$ vs.\ $0.033$), so we do not claim a
four-base sweep against tuned RegMean.

\subsection{Quadratic bridge: does rd-ridge approach the
Fisher-quadratic optimum?}
\label{subsec:exp-e11}

In the limit of small adapter perturbations, the local loss landscape
is near-quadratic and the worst-task-optimal merge under the diagonal
Fisher proxy collapses to the Fisher-magnitude average. This predicts:
shrinking all adapter norms by a common factor $\alpha$, the ratio
$R(\alpha) = \Phi^{\mathrm{ridge}}(\alpha)/\Phi^{\mathrm{Fisher}}(\alpha)$
should approach $1$. We scale every adapter's LoRA \verb|scaling| by
$\alpha$ before merging and run both methods on the $T = 4$ cohort on
two bases spanning the saturation regime.

\paragraph{Yi-1.5-9B-Chat: the prediction holds cleanly.} The ratio
progresses $0.67 \to 0.65 \to 0.73 \to 0.90$ as $\alpha$ shrinks,
landing inside the $[0.8, 1.2]$ window at $\alpha = 0.1$
(Table~\ref{tab:e11-quadbridge}): the rd-encoder ridge solution
approaches the Fisher-quadratic diagonal optimum in the
small-perturbation regime.

\paragraph{Llama-3.1: the bridge has a measurable sweet spot.} The
four-point scan is inconclusive at $\alpha = 0.1$ (excess
$\sim 10^{-4}$, floating-point dominated), so we re-ran with five
additional $\alpha$ values (Table~\ref{tab:e11b-llama-finer}). Below
$\alpha = 0.125$ rd-encoder \emph{over}-saturates the base-model floor
(negative excess; the local-quadratic approximation has broken down on
the other side). In $\alpha \in [0.15, 0.25]$ the ratios are $0.78$,
$0.83$, $0.80$, within $20\%$ of the prediction. Above
$\alpha = 0.25$ the ratio drifts back to $\approx 0.56$ at full scale
as the large-perturbation regime takes over.

\paragraph{What we cautiously claim.} Both bases provide a positive
instance of a quadratic-bridge prediction the lower-bound theorem makes
implicitly: where adapter perturbations are large enough to escape the
ridge-floor and small enough that the local-quadratic model holds, the
rd-encoder ridge merge is within $\sim 20\%$ of the Fisher-quadratic
optimum. The window's location is base-dependent (the small-$\alpha$
limit on compressed-headroom Yi-Chat, an intermediate band on
larger-headroom Llama-Instruct), consistent with the saturation
framing of \S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}. Running the $9$-point scan is
a one-line audit of any new base.
