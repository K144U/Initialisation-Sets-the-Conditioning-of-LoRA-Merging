LoRA fine-tunes are routinely merged into a single deployable
model, but no principled limit governs how well this can be done
at a fixed storage budget. We pose model merging as multi-source
lossy source coding under worst-task distortion and establish
the first rate-distortion theorem for the problem. Our main
tool is an $H_t$-weighted max-$\geq$-avg identity that reduces
worst-task distortion to average-distortion quantization of an
$H_t$-weighted centroid plus an irreducible floor. Under a
Stiefel-random hard distribution with arbitrary task-dependent
curvatures, the floor admits a closed form $B^2(1 - \deff/(Tr))$
in terms of the effective dimension $\deff := \mathbb{E}[
\operatorname{rank}(\sum_t P_{V_t})]$, and the compression term
scales as $\Theta(2^{-2R/\deff})$. We prove a matching
achievability bound via explicit Gaussian-QR random orthogonal
mixing followed by uniform scalar quantization, pinning down the
rate-distortion function $\mathcal{D}^\star = \Theta(B^2 \cdot
2^{-2R/(Tr)})$ up to a universal constant $C = O(T)$ in the
generic floor-zero regime. Monte Carlo experiments validate the
predicted slope to within $\pm 0.01$ across $T\in\{2,3,4,8\}$ and
a range of anisotropic spectra. The bound gives a calibration
curve against which existing and future merging algorithms can
be graded, and explains a recent large-scale empirical finding
that subspace-alignment methods fail at LLM scale when the floor
term is bounded away from zero.
