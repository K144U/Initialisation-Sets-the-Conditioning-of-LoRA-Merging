\subsection{Added baselines and the quadratic bridge}
\label{subsec:exp-e10-e11}

We close \S\ref{sec:experiments} with two compact experiments that
extend the matrix beyond the headline five methods. \S\ref{subsec:exp-e10}
adds two widely-cited LoRA merge baselines (Fisher-magnitude and DELLA);
\S\ref{subsec:exp-e11} tests a prediction implicit in the rd-encoder
construction --- that ridge approaches the Fisher-quadratic diagonal
optimum in the small-perturbation regime.

\subsubsection{Fisher-magnitude and DELLA on the $T=4$ matrix}
\label{subsec:exp-e10}

We add \emph{four} methods commonly requested as merge-paper
baselines: \textbf{Fisher-magnitude} (per-coordinate weighted average
with $F_t[i] = \Delta_t[i]^2$ as the Fisher proxy --- the cheap
non-gradient variant of Matena and Raffel 2022), \textbf{DELLA}
(Deep et al.\ 2024 --- DARE-style random drop with rescale, followed by
TIES density trim and sign election), and the two \emph{data-free
closed-form} merges that are rd-encoder ridge's closest structural
relatives: \textbf{RegMean} (Jin et al.\ 2023, data-free variant using
$A_t^\top A_t$ as the Gram surrogate) and \textbf{AdaMerging}
(Yang et al.\ 2024, data-free variant with per-task
$\alpha \propto \|\Delta_t\|_F$). For each we run a single seed-1 cell
on each of the four bases of \S\ref{subsec:exp-lora} at $T = 4$ with
the same merge cohort, and Table~\ref{tab:e10-baselines} places them in
\emph{one} head-to-head with the published five \emph{and}
rd-encoder ridge --- every method at the same seed and cohort, which is
the comparison the head-to-head claims of \S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}
and the abstract rest on.

The headline result is that \textbf{rd-encoder ridge has the lowest
worst-task excess on every base at these default hyperparameters},
beating its closest structural relative --- data-free RegMean --- by
$0.008$--$0.149$ nats. (When RegMean's own ridge $\lambda$ is tuned,
this margin shrinks and reverses on one base; see
\S\ref{subsec:exp-robustness} and the retraction below.) RegMean is
competitive on the easier bases (Yi, Mistral) but collapses on Llama
($0.243$, worse than TA), and AdaMerging trails throughout; Fisher and
DELLA land in the second tier (ranking $2$--$4$ among the classical
methods on every base, never inverting the
\S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations} R1/R3 ordering).

\paragraph{Cost of these baselines on our setup.}
Fisher-magnitude reduces to an elementwise weighted sum with no
gradient passes (the squared-magnitude proxy is differentiable in
closed form), so its per-cell wallclock is identical to TA on our
infrastructure ($\sim 15$ minutes per base). DELLA adds the DARE
random-drop pass before the TIES trim, increasing cell wallclock by
$\lesssim 5\%$. RegMean and AdaMerging are likewise gradient-free
closed-form merges in their data-free variants, at TA-level cost. None
of the four is a compute barrier to deployment.

\paragraph{What this changes about the practical
recommendations.}
R1 (rd-encoder ridge default) is now \emph{directly} supported in a
single seed-consistent table rather than across two: at default
hyperparameters, rd-encoder ridge has the lowest worst-task excess of
all ten methods on every base, beating data-free RegMean --- its
closest structural relative --- by $0.008$--$0.149$ nats. We are
careful about what this does and does not show. Data-free RegMean has
\emph{no} ridge term, and on Llama it collapses to $0.243$ through the
same sliver-eigenvalue pathology that makes our own un-regularized
encoder blow up to $0.340$ (\S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}). So this
default-hyperparameter comparison establishes that rd-encoder ridge is
a strong merge, but it \emph{conflates} the rate-distortion centroid
with the ridge regularization: part of the Llama gap is simply our
ridge against RegMean's lack of one. We therefore ran the clean test
--- sweeping RegMean's own ridge $\lambda$ over six decades ($10^{-4}$
to $10$) and re-comparing both methods at three matched training seeds
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-robustness}). The conflation matters: with RegMean
tuned to its best $\lambda$ and both methods on \emph{identical}
adapters, rd-encoder ridge still wins on three of four bases (Llama
$0.094$ vs $0.236$; Mistral $0.044$ vs $0.054$; Qwen $0.0097$ vs
$0.0112$, $3$-seed means) --- but \emph{loses} on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat
($0.037$ vs a tuned RegMean's $0.033$, consistent across all three
seeds). We therefore \textbf{retract} any claim that rd-encoder ridge
beats RegMean on \emph{all four} bases: the rate-distortion centroid
carries a real, ridge-independent advantage on three bases, but on the
saturated Yi base --- the one base where we independently flag NLL
excess as an unreliable proxy (\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling} R2) --- a
properly-tuned RegMean edges it. R3 (``TIES or TVQ-$b{=}2$, not
TA'') is preserved: DELLA and
Fisher land between TIES/TVQ-$b{=}2$ and TA on every base, never
inverting the ordering, so we list them as acceptable fallbacks when
rd-encoder ridge is unavailable.

\subsubsection{Quadratic bridge: does rd-ridge approach the
Fisher-quadratic optimum as $\alpha \to 0$?}
\label{subsec:exp-e11}

The rd-encoder ridge construction of \S\ref{subsec:exp-rd-encoder}
solves a regularized least-squares problem on the per-task projector
$V_t$, with regularization $\lambda$ tuned to match the per-base
spectral overlap. In the limit of \emph{small adapter perturbations}
($\|\Delta_t\| \to 0$), the local loss landscape around each
per-task minimum is well-approximated by a quadratic, and the
worst-task-optimal merge under the diagonal Fisher proxy
$F_t[i] = \Delta_t[i]^2$ collapses to the Fisher-magnitude average
of \S\ref{subsec:exp-e10}. This predicts: as we shrink all adapter
norms by a common factor $\alpha$, the ratio
$\Phi^{\mathrm{ridge}}(\alpha) / \Phi^{\mathrm{Fisher}}(\alpha)$
should approach $1$.

\paragraph{Experimental setup.} We scale every loaded adapter's LoRA
per-layer \verb|scaling| attribute by $\alpha \in
\{0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0\}$ before merging (a uniform multiplicative
shrink on $\Delta_t$ across all tasks and projections), then run
both methods on the standard $T = 4$ cohort on two bases:
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Yi-1.5-9B-Chat. The bases span the
saturation regime: Llama-Instruct's per-task headroom is moderate;
Yi-Chat's is compressed (\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}).
Table~\ref{tab:e11-quadbridge} reports worst-task NLL excess at
each scale and the ratio $R(\alpha) = \Phi^{\mathrm{ridge}}(\alpha)
/ \Phi^{\mathrm{Fisher}}(\alpha)$.

\paragraph{Verdict on Yi-1.5-9B-Chat.}
The prediction holds cleanly: the ratio progresses $0.67 \to 0.65 \to
0.73 \to 0.90$ as $\alpha$ shrinks from $1.0$ to $0.1$, landing
inside the $[0.8, 1.2]$ window at $\alpha = 0.1$. The rd-encoder
ridge solution is empirically approaching the Fisher-quadratic
diagonal optimum in the small-perturbation regime, consistent with
the local-quadratic interpretation of the lower bound
(\S\ref{sec:lower_bound}).

\paragraph{Verdict on Llama-3.1: the bridge has a measurable
sweet spot.}
The initial four-point scan of Table~\ref{tab:e11-quadbridge} was
inconclusive on Llama because $\alpha = 0.1$ produced rd-encoder
worst-task excess $\sim 10^{-4}$, dominated by floating-point noise.
We re-ran with five additional $\alpha$ values in
$\{0.05, 0.075, 0.125, 0.15, 0.20\}$. The combined scan
(Table~\ref{tab:e11b-llama-finer}) shows the bridge is real, but
restricted to an intermediate $\alpha$ window:
\begin{itemize}
\item Below $\alpha = 0.125$: rd-encoder ridge \emph{over}-saturates
  the base-model floor, producing negative worst-task excess (the
  merged model has lower NLL than the per-task minima themselves).
  The ratio is meaningless in this regime; the bridge does not apply
  because the local-quadratic approximation has already broken down
  on the other side.
\item $\alpha \in [0.15, 0.25]$ ---  the prediction window: ratios
  are $0.78, 0.83$ (\emph{in window}), $0.80$. The rd-encoder ridge
  solution tracks the Fisher-quadratic optimum to within $20\%$
  here.
\item Above $\alpha = 0.25$: ratios drift back down to
$\approx 0.56$ at full scale $\alpha = 1.0$, as the per-task
adapters are large enough that the local-quadratic approximation no
longer dominates the merge cost.
\end{itemize}
The bridge holds; it occupies a measurable window rather than the
asymptotic limit. We read this as: \emph{the rd-encoder ridge
construction is a tight approximation to the Fisher-quadratic merge
in the regime where adapter perturbations are large enough to escape
the base-floor regularization yet small enough that the
local-quadratic loss model still holds.} The window is base-dependent
--- it occupies the small-$\alpha$ limit on Yi-Chat and a mid-range
window $[0.15, 0.25]$ on Llama-Instruct --- and is again consistent
with the saturation framing of \S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}.

\paragraph{Why the cross-base $\alpha$ window differs.}
The two bases place the bridge window at different $\alpha$. On
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat the per-task headroom is compressed
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}), so even $\alpha = 1.0$ keeps the
merged adapter inside the local-quadratic regime, and the bridge is
visible in the small-$\alpha$ limit. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct the
larger headroom means the local-quadratic regime occupies an
\emph{intermediate}-$\alpha$ band ($[0.15, 0.25]$) bracketed by
ridge-floor over-saturation below and the large-perturbation
regime above. We read this as a base-specific manifestation of the
same phenomenon: the bridge is real on both bases, but the
$\alpha$ where it is visible is set by the base's headroom.

\paragraph{What we cautiously claim.}
Both bases (Yi-1.5-9B-Chat in the small-$\alpha$ limit,
Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct in the intermediate-$\alpha$ window $[0.15,
0.25]$) provide a positive instance of a quadratic-bridge prediction
that the lower-bound theorem (\S\ref{sec:lower_bound}) makes
implicitly: in the regime where adapter perturbations are large
enough to escape the ridge-floor and small enough that the
local-quadratic loss model holds, the rd-encoder ridge merge
converges to within $\sim 20\%$ of the Fisher-quadratic optimum that
the bound predicts is asymptotically tight. The location of the
bridge window is base-dependent and consistent with the
saturation framing of \S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}; identifying the
window as a one-line audit of any new base (run the $9$-point scan
of Table~\ref{tab:e11b-llama-finer}) is the practical takeaway.
