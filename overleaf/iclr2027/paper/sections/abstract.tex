We prove the first rate-distortion lower bound for LoRA model merging:
worst-task NLL excess splits into an \emph{irreducible floor} determined
by per-task weight-subspace overlap and a \emph{compression term} that
decays exponentially in the bit budget, with a matching achievable upper
bound via Hadamard-incoherent orthogonal mixing followed by scalar
quantization. On a $4$-base $\times$ $7$-method $\times$ $2$-downstream-metric
matrix spanning Llama-3.1-Instruct, Mistral-7B-v0.3, Qwen-2.5-7B, and
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat at task counts $T \in \{2, 4, 7\}$, the floor is exactly
zero on every base --- current methods leave $0.10$--$0.22$ nats/token of
worst-task excess as \emph{algorithmic slack, not an information-theoretic
limit}. A regularized variant of our encoder (\textsc{rd-encoder ridge})
ties or beats every published method at $T = 4$ and widens its lead at
$T = 7$ (rd-ridge / Task-Arithmetic excess ratio $0.84 \to 0.68 \to 0.52$
on Llama-3.1-Instruct), with NLL-based recommendations confirmed on GSM8K
exact-match and HumanEval pass@1 across $7$ of $8$ (base $\times$ metric)
cells. A measurable base-saturation regime --- diagnosable from a
$1$-CPU-minute audit of any trained adapter cohort --- predicts when
classical methods such as TIES invert from best to worst, and motivates a
five-rule decision tree distilling the empirical results into deployable
guidance.
