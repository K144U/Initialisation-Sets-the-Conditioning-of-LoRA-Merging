\subsection{Multi-seed robustness of the method ordering}
\label{subsec:exp-multiseed-bootstrap}

The headline ordering of the $T = 4$ matrix
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed}) and the $T$-scaling sweep
(\S\ref{subsec:exp-T-scaling}) are stated as point estimates on a
single training seed per adapter cell. We here verify that the
ordering is robust to seed variance by retraining the $T = 4$ matrix
adapters with three independent seeds ($r = 16$, identical
hyperparameters, seeds $\{1, 2, 3\}$) and computing $10{,}000$-resample
paired bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals on the five key
method-pair gaps that drive the recommendations of
\S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}.

\paragraph{Decision rules.} For a method-pair gap
$\Delta = \Phi^\star_A - \Phi^\star_B$ measured in nats/token at
each seed, we label the pair as
\begin{itemize}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Confident}: the $95\%$ paired bootstrap CI for $\Delta$
        does not cross zero (the sign of the gap is seed-stable).
  \item \textbf{Borderline}: CI crosses zero but
        $|\Delta| > \tfrac{1}{2}$ of the CI half-width.
  \item \textbf{Inconclusive}: CI crosses zero \emph{and}
        $|\Delta| \leq \tfrac{1}{2}$ of the CI half-width.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:multiseed-bootstrap} reports the
five gaps on each of the four bases. \emph{Every one of the $20$
method-pair gaps is Confident.} The signs of the gaps that determine
the \S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations} R1 / R3 ordering ---
TIES or TVQ-$b{=}2$ vs.\ TA / DARE / KnOTS --- agree on every base
and survive a paired bootstrap that bounds away from zero. The
largest CI half-width across the $20$ gaps is $\pm 0.0044$
nats/token (Qwen TA$-$TVQ$_2$); the tightest-margin gap is
$|\Delta_{\mathrm{ties-tvq_2}}| = 0.0076$ on Mistral-7B, whose $95\%$
CI $[-0.0099, -0.0043]$ still excludes zero. On
Yi-1.5-9B-Chat, TIES beats TVQ-$b{=}2$ by $0.0136 \pm 0.0010$, a gap
the original single-seed numbers under-reported.

\paragraph{Caveats.} The seeds vary only the LoRA training
initialization and data-shuffle order; the base model and
hyperparameters are held fixed. Cross-base seed-noise distributions
will differ if the base's instruction-tuning recipe is itself stable
across seeds, which the original base authors do not publish. We
interpret the multi-seed bootstrap as quantifying the
\emph{noise-floor} on our method-pair comparisons, not as a full
generalization claim across pretraining recipes.

\paragraph{What this changes.} Every R1/R3-relevant method-pair gap
stays Confident under the full 3-seed paired bootstrap, and the
qualitative ordering of \S\ref{subsec:exp-multiseed} is unaltered. The
five-rule decision tree of \S\ref{sec:practical-recommendations}
therefore rests on confident multi-seed gaps, not on a single-seed
coincidence. This closes the seed-robustness concern catalogued in
\S\ref{sec:discussion} (limitations item 3).
